## AI visibility is not an automatic extension of SEO

[Freshpet ranked well in traditional search and had established brand awareness in the pet food category](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-freshpet-earned-ais-trust-a-geo-ai-visibility-playbook-recap/588706/), yet the brand was nearly invisible when AI Overviews and LLMs began reshaping product discovery. This gap reveals a critical fact: a recognized brand and strong SEO foundation do not guarantee relevance to AI-generated answers. Large language models understand industries, topics, and entity relationships differently than search engines rank pages. The available content may not clearly establish how a brand relates to a user's specific question, leaving the brand absent from recommendations even when it deserves to be there.

The lesson is immediate. Stop assuming AI visibility will follow from traditional rankings or brand awareness.

## Start with observation, not assumption

The first step in building AI visibility is to ask the questions your customers actually ask, then record what the systems return. Where is your brand missing? Where is it misrepresented, or supported only by outdated information? [Freshpet's team learned this discipline by moving beyond generic advice about "writing for AI"](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-freshpet-earned-ais-trust-a-geo-ai-visibility-playbook-recap/588706/) and instead auditing the specific gaps in their AI visibility. This approach works because it grounds strategy in evidence rather than assumption.

Our recommendation: build a working list of prompts your audience is likely to use. Start with fifty if you have the capacity, then grow the list over time. Group them into topic clusters so you can spot patterns. Which clusters return weak answers? Which ones mention competitors but not you?

## Restructure content to be quotable, not just readable

Much of Freshpet's legacy content was written like a magazine article, drawing readers through a narrative from beginning to end. Readers found value in that flow, but key facts buried in long paragraphs are harder for an LLM to extract. The answer is not to strip away brand voice or write awkward copy for machines. Instead, organize complex ideas into focused sections that can stand on their own.

The practical steps are straightforward:

- Use descriptive headings that signal what each section answers
- Write short paragraphs that support one idea at a time
- Place direct answers high, before elaboration
- Use meaningful lists to break up dense information
- Apply appropriate schema markup to help machines parse structure

[The shift is toward writing content that can be quoted, not merely read.](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-freshpet-earned-ais-trust-a-geo-ai-visibility-playbook-recap/588706/) For Freshpet, that meant revisiting page templates, cleaning up HTML, and making it easier to apply schema. The content still sounds natural. It just respects the constraints that make information machine-readable.

See [how to structure content so AI answers can actually use it](/blog/structure-content-for-ai-answers) for a detailed breakdown of headings, lists, and section architecture.

## Audit the rendered experience, not just editorial inventory

A valuable piece of content can exist on a page and still remain difficult for crawlers to access. [Freshpet discovered that product reviews and Q&A content delivered through JavaScript were not readily visible to LLMs.](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-freshpet-earned-ais-trust-a-geo-ai-visibility-playbook-recap/588706/) The takeaway extends beyond this one technical issue: audit what crawlers actually see when they render your page, not what you know exists in your content library.

Check the following:

- Crawler controls and robots.txt directives that may block AI user-agents
- JavaScript dependencies that delay or prevent rendering of key content
- Page speed and whether slow loading affects crawler completion
- Whether the most important answers are available in parseable HTML
- Whether schema markup is present and valid

Our recommendation: [use a free audit](/free-audit) to see which parts of your site are accessible to AI crawlers, then prioritize moving critical content out of JavaScript rendering and into the HTML payload.

## Authority comes from context, not just links

[A mention in a major publication has value, but an industry-specific source often does more to establish a brand's relationship to a specialized topic.](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-freshpet-earned-ais-trust-a-geo-ai-visibility-playbook-recap/588706/) A credible pet industry publication can reinforce expertise in ways a broad national outlet may not, because LLMs learn what a brand is known for from the editorial context of its mentions.

The surrounding coverage matters too. If every mention uses the brand name as anchor text and points to the homepage, LLMs receive less context about which specific subjects the brand should be associated with. Earned media works harder when the article itself accurately explains the brand's expertise and points to a relevant onsite resource. That link teaches the model what topic the brand should be credited for.

This is a hypothesis that still merits testing: whether LLMs weight mentions more heavily when the linking article demonstrates clear topical relevance to the destination page. What we can verify is that [mentions and citations are not distributed evenly across your brand's topic areas](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-freshpet-earned-ais-trust-a-geo-ai-visibility-playbook-recap/588706/), and intentional work to build topical authority through earned media delivers better results than hoping for undirected coverage.

## Listen to communities, then build evidence-backed content

Reddit and other community platforms matter because customers use them to exchange candid opinions, and LLMs can draw from those conversations. But that does not mean jumping into every thread. [Freshpet takes a listening-first approach: the team monitors for recurring questions or misconceptions, then creates authoritative content elsewhere to address them.](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-freshpet-earned-ais-trust-a-geo-ai-visibility-playbook-recap/588706/)

Community monitoring is not only reputation management. It becomes an input for content strategy. When the same concern appears repeatedly, marketers can build a clear, evidence-backed resource that customers and AI systems can both find. The brand avoids direct arguments in threads (which rarely change minds) and instead creates a trusted reference that serves everyone.

## Track prompts and organize them into topic clusters

[Freshpet's team was tracking 475 prompts across major LLMs, grouped into topic categories.](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-freshpet-earned-ais-trust-a-geo-ai-visibility-playbook-recap/588706/) That structure lets the team spot clusters of weak answers, create content for related questions, and involve subject matter experts where their credentials can strengthen the response. The point is not to copy that exact prompt count. It is to build a set broad enough to represent your customer journey, then organize it so the findings lead to specific work.

[Stated as a hypothesis](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-freshpet-earned-ais-trust-a-geo-ai-visibility-playbook-recap/588706/), a brand that tracks and categorizes prompts will spot content gaps faster and with more precision than one that relies on general SEO metrics or traffic patterns. This aligns with what we know: [SEO metrics and GEO metrics do not carry over cleanly](/blog/seo-vs-geo-metrics). Impressions in traditional search do not predict citation in AI answers. You need metrics designed for how LLMs work.

## The work is never finished

AI visibility does not map neatly onto the old model of ranking, click, and conversion. As systems evolve and customer questions shift, the prompts you tracked six months ago may no longer represent your audience's behavior. The content structure that works today may not be optimal next quarter.

Freshpet's approach treats GEO as an ongoing program rather than a one-time audit. That mindset—observation, structured content, technical accessibility, contextual authority, community insight, and disciplined measurement—is what earns and keeps AI's trust.
