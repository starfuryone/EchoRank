## Pages that lead with answers get cited 4 to 5 times more often

[According to Search Engine Journal's analysis of 193,507 AI answers](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-cited-pages-more-often-got-straight-to-the-answer/594781/), when AI systems pull pages directly into their responses, those cited pages open with a direct answer to the user's question far more often than pages the AI passes over. The difference is stark: cited pages led with an answer 20% of the time compared to just 5% for pages Google's AI never retrieved, and 23% versus 14% when comparing cited pages to pages that appeared in the same AI response but weren't quoted.

The research comes from TJ Robertson of TJ Digital, who tracked 4,167 different questions across eight weekly snapshots between August and October 2026. His team used blind raters to score 195 page pairs without knowing which had been cited, isolating answer placement as a measurable factor.

## Structure matters more than most SEO signals

Answer placement dominated the results. Beyond the headline finding, cited pages rated significantly higher for completeness (2.3 on a 1-to-5 scale versus 1.7 for passed-over pages). About half of pages the AI skipped never contained an answer in the portion raters reviewed; only 23% of cited pages had that problem.

Our recommendation: if your content is meant to be found by AI systems, test moving your answer closer to the page opening. [Structuring content for AI answers](/blog/structure-content-for-ai-answers) is not about burying detail—it's about sequencing: answer first, then evidence and elaboration.

Other traits associated with citation included freshness and recency. Pages updated within 12 months showed 88% higher odds of being cited. A publication year in the page title carried more than twice the odds. Tables appeared in cited pages more frequently (69% higher odds). First-hand experience or credentials registered a 45% boost.

What surprised Robertson's team: author bylines correlated with 28% *lower* odds of citation, and calls-to-action with 12% lower odds. Robertson states he cannot explain these associations and does not recommend removing either element based on this finding alone. A hypothesis worth testing in your own data before acting on it.

## Schema markup showed modest gains; other factors diverged from earlier research

Schema markup—specifically structured data—associated with 13% higher odds of citation. This aligns with but does not confirm earlier Ahrefs research showing cited pages were three times as likely to contain JSON-LD. When Ahrefs tested adding JSON-LD to previously uncited pages, the citation increase was not statistically significant. We cannot yet verify whether schema markup drives citations or whether pages that already rank and cite well tend to have better markup.

Promotional tone was nearly identical between cited and passed-over pages. Three-quarters of all pages tested were promoting their own products or services, and both groups rated around 3.7 to 3.8 on tone—meaning the distinction between cited and uncited content was not whether it was promotional, but whether it answered the question first.

The analysis controlled for domain authority (Ahrefs Domain Rating) and page type, reporting results as odds ratios rather than raw percentages. This matters because it means the patterns held across different site sizes and content formats.

## What this means for your content audit

Before adding new signals to your site, audit where your current answers sit on the page:

- Does your product/service page answer the buyer's question in the first 100 words?
- If you have comparison or overview content, does the opening sentence or paragraph give a direct answer?
- Are your updates and publish dates visible to AI crawlers?
- Do you use data, tables, or structured information early rather than late?
- Is your content a mix of promotional pitch and substantive answer, or mostly one?

## The relationship between citation and actual quality remains unclear

A caveat worth highlighting: [Robertson states](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-cited-pages-more-often-got-straight-to-the-answer/594781/) he views his findings as showing associations rather than proof that answer placement causes citation. Blind raters found that roughly one-quarter of cited pages still did not provide a clear answer to the question in the portion they read. This suggests AI systems are pulling pages for reasons beyond pure answer quality—link authority, domain reputation, crawl recency, and unknown ranking factors all play a role.

The research also tracked only one agency's questions across their specific client base. The patterns may hold for your questions too, or they may not. A hypothesis: content traits that work for one business model or industry may not transfer equally to another. The study notes coverage across nine different business models, but the weighting and mix of questions within each is not detailed.

## How this fits into your AI citation strategy

[Finding your AI citation gap](/blog/find-your-ai-citation-gap)—the gap between being mentioned and being quoted—requires comparing how often your brand or pages appear in AI answers versus how often they are attributed. Placement of your answer is one lever. But you also need:

- Freshness signals (publication or update dates the crawler can see)
- Completeness (answering the question fully, not partially)
- Specific expertise or data others lack
- Crawlability and structured data where appropriate

[Why AI recommends your competitors](/blog/why-ai-recommends-your-competitors) sometimes comes down to these exact factors: they structure their answer upfront, they keep their content fresh, and they do the work of making their pages readable to both crawlers and models.

Robertson's data set draws from ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, and Google AI Overviews—three different AI systems with different ranking and retrieval logic. We cannot yet verify whether all three weight answer placement equally or whether the signal is stronger in some systems than others. Testing on your own questions, with tools that track AI citations across multiple models, will tell you whether this pattern holds for your content and audience.
