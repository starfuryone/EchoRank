The top 10 websites in the U.S. account for 68.6% of all traffic to the top 150 sites, up from 68.8% a year ago. Concentration is tightening, not loosening. Yet beneath that headline, [according to Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/winners-losers-us-website-traffic-past-year-486843), something far stranger has happened: visitors are arriving through channels that look like growth but actually signal a measurement crisis. The winners are obvious. The real story is what nobody can measure anymore.

## Google and YouTube grew, but traffic stayed concentrated

The top websites haven't reshuffled much since last year. [Search Engine Land's analysis](https://searchengineland.com/winners-losers-us-website-traffic-past-year-486843) of Semrush Traffic Analytics data through July 2026 shows that if you only looked at the rankings, you'd think nothing had changed. You'd be wrong.

Google's traffic rose 10.72% year over year. YouTube rose 36.6%—a startling gain that suggests video consumption continues to accelerate. Together with the third-place site, they now generate 54.3% of the top 10's traffic. The concentration at the very top has intensified. The web isn't becoming less dominated by a handful of platforms as AI changes search. It's becoming more so.

Our recommendation: [track your own visibility across these concentrated traffic sources](/free-audit). Monitor where you appear relative to competitors in both organic and AI-generated search results. That's how you'll see whether you're winning share inside the platforms where the audience actually is.

## ChatGPT.com entered the top 20, while Bing collapsed

If one number captures the search landscape shift, [it's this](https://searchengineland.com/winners-losers-us-website-traffic-past-year-486843): ChatGPT.com is up 48.38% year over year and now ranks ninth among all U.S. websites with 1.09 billion monthly visits. A year ago, that would have seemed unthinkable. Today, an AI chatbot outranks DuckDuckGo and Bing—search engines that have been operating since 2008 and 2009.

Generative AI usage has moved from novelty to habit fast enough to show up as a top 20 traffic gain. TikTok, by comparison, ranks 14th and gained only 1.63% year over year. This isn't a story about "new platforms" winning across the board. It's specifically a story about chatbot adoption at scale.

Microsoft's three-year, high-budget bet to integrate Copilot into Bing hasn't reversed the trajectory. [Bing is down 50.43% year over year](https://searchengineland.com/winners-losers-us-website-traffic-past-year-486843)—the steepest decline among the top 20 sites. Bing's standalone search share has been flat against Google for years, and this year's numbers look like a continuation of that pattern, not a break from it.

We recommend examining [why your brand may not be recommended by AI systems](/blog/why-ai-recommends-your-competitors) and what competitive signals those systems are reading instead. If traffic is shifting to chatbot interfaces, your visibility in those systems requires deliberate, different strategies than traditional search.

## Direct traffic is surging unmeasured, hiding the real AI shift

Here's where the story becomes urgent. [According to the case study in Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/winners-losers-us-website-traffic-past-year-486843), a niche publishing website showed impressive year-over-year growth: sessions up 105%, engaged sessions up 111%, key events up 80.5%. The first read looks like a victory.

But when you examine the traffic acquisition report more closely, direct traffic is up 157% and now accounts for 78% of total sessions, up from 62.5% a year earlier. GA4 can't identify where four out of five visitors came from. The unattributed share is growing every month.

"Direct" isn't a traffic source—it's the label GA4 assigns to everything it cannot attribute. An increasing amount of that unattributed traffic is almost certainly arriving via links generated inside AI chat interfaces, which routinely strip the referrer data that GA4 depends on. The publisher's historical metrics look fantastic, but the attribution underneath is degrading every month, making the next set of content and channel investment decisions unreliable.

This is a hypothesis worth testing: you may be receiving significant traffic from AI chat systems and never know it because the referrer data is lost in transit. The growth looks real in GA4. The attribution doesn't.

## What the traffic data can and cannot tell you

The winners and losers list captures real shifts in where U.S. web traffic is flowing. Google and YouTube are consolidating reach. ChatGPT.com has become a top 20 destination. Bing's bet hasn't worked. Those are facts grounded in site visit counts.

What the traffic data cannot tell you is this: How much of your growth comes from AI systems? How much are your competitors winning through visibility you can't measure? Are you appearing in AI-generated answers, and does that drive attribution or does it just disappear into direct traffic?

[Google Analytics 4 became mandatory on July 1, 2023](https://searchengineland.com/winners-losers-us-website-traffic-past-year-486843), right when the industry lost a clean measurement baseline. Less than a year later, AI Overviews began rolling out to U.S. users. The timing was unforgiving. By the time most teams learned GA4's new interface, the measurement of search traffic was already degrading as AI systems replaced human-driven referrer attribution.

## How to recover visibility where measurement is broken

You cannot solve attribution through GA4 alone. Our recommendation is to measure AI visibility directly through sources that track citation rather than click-through.

- Monitor where your brand appears in AI chat responses and answer engine results
- Use citation tracking to identify which of your pages AI systems actually cite in responses
- Compare citation volume to your organic search rankings to find the gap
- Track competitor citations to see which positioning strategies are working at scale
- Measure direct traffic against content publication dates to find patterns that GA4 obscures

The top 10 U.S. websites are more dominant than ever. But that dominance now includes platforms that strip attribution from the majority of their referrer links. Your traffic is growing. Your ability to explain where it comes from is shrinking. The winners and losers of the past year are clear. What's unclear is whether you're actually winning or just watching a measurement crisis hide the truth.
