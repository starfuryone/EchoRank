Your site ranks on page one for your target keyword. You ask ChatGPT or Claude the same question. Neither mentions you. Your competitors appear instead, even though the SERP says you should.

This is not a ranking problem. It is a citation problem. [According to Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/the-ai-citation-mistake-optimizing-for-google-not-for-sources-webinar/588522/), a ranking is earned through Google's algorithm, but a citation is earned through LLM validation—and those are two entirely different systems.

Position tracking will not explain the discrepancy. The sources LLMs cite are ones they have independently retrieved and trusted to validate answers. That set of sources rarely matches the top of the SERP. Which domains LLMs cite most, and how that mix changes from ChatGPT to Perplexity to Gemini, depends on industry category rather than search visibility.

Our recommendation is to stop treating AI visibility and SERP visibility as the same problem. They require different strategies.

## Why SERP rankings and AI citations are different systems

A ranking is a position on Google's search results page, earned through SEO signals Google measures. A citation is a mention or quote in an LLM's answer, earned because that LLM has decided your source is trustworthy enough to include.

Citation profiles differ by industry. Some categories have a small, predictable set of domains that LLMs cite repeatedly. That set rarely overlaps with the sites ranking highest on Google for the same query. For example, an LLM answering a health question may cite trusted medical databases rather than a well-optimized blog post that ranks first. A financial question may return citations from specialized financial platforms, not the highest-authority general news sites.

The mix also shifts between models. ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini do not cite identical sources. Each has trained on different data and may weight source credibility differently. As a hypothesis, the weights may also shift as each model updates its training or changes how it validates answers.

Our recommendation is to track your mentions [across multiple LLMs](/blog/track-brand-mentions-across-llms), not just one. A single model's citation behavior tells you only part of the story.

## Why ranking page one is not a citation strategy

Pitching to LLMs works differently than pitching to Google. Google's crawlers index your site based on content quality, links, and technical signals. LLMs cite sources based on what they learned during training and how they validate answers at inference time. Those are not the same criteria.

[According to Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/the-ai-citation-mistake-optimizing-for-google-not-for-sources-webinar/588522/), getting published by citation-heavy domains is a pitching problem more than a technical SEO problem. Some accept contributed articles. Some require a listing and verified reviews before your brand appears at all. Others are communities where the account posting matters as much as the post.

The requirements differ by source type:

- Contributed article platforms may accept pitches directly, but approval takes time
- Listing-and-review platforms require business verification and customer reviews before citation visibility
- Community sites depend on account reputation and participation history, not just a single post
- Industry databases or directories may need formal submission and compliance with their schema or data standards

Each source type has its own approval timeline and pathway to visibility in LLM outputs. One may show results in weeks; another may take months. There is no one-size-fits-all approach.

Our recommendation is to identify the citation sources LLMs actually trust in your industry, then map the pitching requirements for each. Prioritize the ones with the shortest approval timeline and easiest entry barrier first.

## How to identify your industry's citation sources

This is where most teams run into friction. You cannot guess which sources matter for AI citations. You have to audit them.

[Search Engine Journal reports](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/the-ai-citation-mistake-optimizing-for-google-not-for-sources-webinar/588522/) that per-industry citation breakdowns are available, with data on which sources each LLM cites most, grouped by category. No generic list will work; a hospitality site's citation sources look nothing like a software site's.

To start, conduct a citation audit:

- Ask each major LLM (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini) a representative question in your space
- Note which domains appear in the answers
- Repeat across several questions to spot patterns
- Separate SERP rankings from citation counts—you are looking for who appears in LLM answers, not who ranks on Google
- Compare the results across models to see where citations diverge

You can also use [Echorank's free audit tool](/free-audit) to identify gaps between your SERP presence and your citation presence across models.

Once you have your list of citation sources, research their contribution policies. Do they accept guest posts? Do they require listings and reviews? Are they syndication platforms? Each pathway has different time and effort costs.

## What happens after you identify the sources

Getting cited is not instantaneous. Unlike SEO, where a page can rank within weeks of publishing, LLM citations depend on whether that source is part of the model's training data or whether the model retrieves it at query time.

A hypothesis: models trained on data through a cutoff date will cite domains that were prominent in that snapshot. Newer sources may be cited through retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), where the model fetches sources fresh at query time. But we cannot yet verify whether each major model relies more on training data or live retrieval, or how the balance shifts as models update.

What we do know is that timing matters. Getting into a source that LLMs cite regularly is not automatic. You need the source to be:

- Present in the model's training data (if the model uses cutoff-date training)
- Actively retrieved by the model at query time (if it uses RAG)
- Recognized as credible enough to include in an answer

The fastest path is usually to contribute to or get listed in a source that LLMs already cite heavily in your industry. You inherit some of that trust immediately. Building a brand new domain to citation visibility takes much longer.

Our recommendation is to audit your citation gap before you do anything else. See /blog/find-your-ai-citation-gap for a detailed method.

## Next steps: audit, prioritize, pitch

Stop optimizing solely for SERP position. Your ranking does not predict your AI citation rate.

Here is the order most teams should follow:

- Audit which sources LLMs cite most in your category
- Compare that list to the sites ranking on page one for your keywords
- Note the gaps (sites you rank for but LLMs do not cite)
- Research the pitching requirements for each high-citation source
- Build a phased pitch plan, starting with the lowest-friction entry points
- Set up tracking to measure citation visibility as you gain placement

This is not SEO. This is source relationship building. Your goal is not to rank; it is to become a source that LLMs validate and cite.
