Google's AI Overviews answer questions directly on the results page, shortening the journey from search to decision. Most paid search teams still bid as if the page hasn't changed. [According to Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/ai-overviews-paid-search-fight-influence-generate-demand-488451), that assumption no longer holds. The value of a paid click now depends on what the user was really looking for. A single bid lever—bid up on strong performance, bid down on weak—can't solve three different problems at once. Instead, separate your keywords into three buckets: Fight, Influence, and Generate Demand. Each one describes a different outcome on the page and calls for a different approach.

## Fight: Defend the bottom of the funnel where clicks still convert

The Fight bucket is for bottom-of-funnel keywords where commercial intent is clear and clicks still turn into leads and sales. These are product searches plus modifiers, supplier shortlists, "buy," "quote," "distributor," and brand terms combined with buying intent. Here, the core job hasn't changed much. You want to win the auction that sits above the AI Overview answer, and you want the ad itself to answer the buying question. [A July SE Ranking study of commercial queries in Google's U.S. AI Mode found that ad appearance likelihood rose steadily with cost per click](https://searchengineland.com/ai-overviews-paid-search-fight-influence-generate-demand-488451). Where the market already pays more, AI-era ad surfaces appear more often. About seven in 10 answers with an ad showed two advertisers in the block, so you rarely compete alone.

Fight is expensive for a reason. Treat it that way. Don't pour Fight budget into informational queries the Overview has already answered. Our recommendation is to identify which of your bottom-funnel keywords still generate qualified conversions after the Overview rolled out. If the data holds, aggressive bidding still works. If it doesn't, you may have misclassified the query.

## Influence: Get cited inside the answer instead of bidding for a click

Influence queries are those where the AI Overview or a longer conversational answer does the educating. Often, the user won't click at all. The goal shifts from capturing a cheap visit to being part of how the answer gets built: cited as a source or presented as one of the brands named inside the answer. The best ads must be answers. In an Influence query, your ad brief starts from the question the user is really asking, rather than from a keyword list. That means SEO and PPC address the same challenge, not competing against each other.

[B2B and industrial accounts see this most clearly](https://searchengineland.com/ai-overviews-paid-search-fight-influence-generate-demand-488451). In one account, "learn and solve" and some "informational" terms were the ones surfacing AI answer blocks. For those queries, plan for share of the answer instead of maximum CPC. Our recommendation is to work very closely with your SEO team on Influence keywords. Usually, you're not interested in paying to appear where organic is already winning. Being visible twice, in paid and in the answer, barely happens anyway. When data pushes you to move budget into Fight and Generate Demand, deliberately avoid being aggressive on terms where SEO already earns the visibility. That keeps paid spend pointed at the gaps you're interested in covering.

To strengthen your chances of being cited in AI answers, [structure your content so AI models can actually extract and use it](/blog/structure-content-for-ai-answers). The clearer your answer to the question being asked, the more likely you are to be included.

## Generate Demand: Rebuild top-of-funnel awareness when zero-click wins

Generate Demand is for queries where zero-click has already won and paid search alone won't rebuild the top of the funnel. Informational demand that used to create assisted journeys now often ends on Google, with no visit to anyone. This work is about recognizing that some keywords have stopped being capture channels and have become a signal of a demand gap. The capture still happens later, in Fight, once the person is ready to act.

Generate Demand fills the pool that Fight then fishes from. Measured only on last-click Search ROAS, this work always looks weak because its payoff appears as brand searches and better-qualified pipeline weeks later. [Paid search alone won't show the full picture](https://searchengineland.com/ai-overviews-paid-search-fight-influence-generate-demand-488451). The real value shows up in brand lift, assisted conversions, and the quality of prospects entering your pipeline. Our recommendation is to track these keywords separately from Fight and measure them on brand search volume and pipeline quality rather than direct ROAS.

## The three ways AI Overviews change the economics of paid search

When users get a usable answer without leaving Google, both organic and paid lose clicks. The effect is stronger when your brand isn't mentioned in the AI Overview. The clicks that remain tend to be better qualified, but the drop in volume usually exceeds the lift in quality, so total conversions decline overall.

There are fewer premium positions at the top. More advertisers are shifting their budgets to paid search to offset lost visibility. The pressure is strongest in B2B and services, and on comparative queries such as "best X" or "X reviews." Pure transactional queries hold up better because users still need to click through to buy or request a quote.

[There are often only a few useful slots above the AI Overview or AI Mode answer](https://searchengineland.com/ai-overviews-paid-search-fight-influence-generate-demand-488451). Falling below the Overview can sharply reduce click-through, especially on mobile. Absolute Top impression share and impression share lost to rank become especially useful for understanding that pressure.

## How to reclassify your account

Underneath these changes is a shift in behavior. People can research and decide within a single answer experience, shortening the window to capture and persuade them. Intent still lives in your account, but the economics attached to each type of intent have changed. Classify your query before you touch the bid.

- List all keywords driving conversions in the last 90 days
- Search each one and note whether an AI Overview appears
- If yes, determine whether the Overview answers the user's question or points them toward more research
- If it answers the question, classify as Influence or Generate Demand
- If it doesn't fully answer, and users still click to buy, classify as Fight
- For each bucket, set separate bid targets and measure separate outcomes
- Review every 30 days as the Overview changes

Our recommendation is to use [impression share and impression share lost to rank](/free-tools) to surface which Fight keywords are being pushed below the Overview. That metric becomes more important now that real estate above the answer is scarcer.

## What you still don't know about AI Overview visibility

A hypothesis worth testing: the overlap between being cited in an AI Overview and receiving paid clicks. [We cannot yet verify whether appearing in an answer block reduces the likelihood of a paid click from the same user](/blog/find-your-ai-citation-gap), or whether it increases brand awareness enough to show up in other keywords later. The sources report the pattern, but not the mechanism. Track your own brand searches and assisted conversions to build that picture in your account. Over time, you'll know which of your keywords have shifted buckets and why.
