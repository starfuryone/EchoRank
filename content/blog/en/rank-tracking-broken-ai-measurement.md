Yes, rank tracking broke. [According to Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/rank-tracking-broke-in-september-the-future-is-measured-by-brand-revenue/592783/), Google made aggressive moves in September to prevent third parties from scraping its SERPs, causing simultaneous failures across dozens of tracking tools and affecting tens of thousands of SEO agencies. The outages came alongside delayed data processing in Google Search Console. But the real problem is larger: traditional keyword rankings no longer measure what matters. In AI-driven search, a brand can appear in an AI answer—a direct citation that builds trust—while ranking tools report a position drop. The future belongs to teams measuring brand visibility across all SERP surfaces and AI citations, then connecting those metrics to actual revenue.

## What Happened: Google Closed the Data Valve

[Search Engine Journal reports](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/rank-tracking-broke-in-september-the-future-is-measured-by-brand-revenue/592783/) that Google changed its HTML structure in ways designed to stop third-party tools from extracting SERP data for competitive advantage. The breakdowns were sudden and simultaneous across the industry, indicating a coordinated shift rather than isolated outages. This followed the September spam update, which itself had implications for data noise in tracking systems.

The impact was widespread. Dozens, possibly hundreds, of marketing and SEO tools went dark or degraded. Tens of thousands of internal teams and agencies lost access to the standardized rank reports they've relied on for over a decade. Outages have continued into October, with some data delays still affecting Google Search Console itself.

This is not a temporary blip. The data supply chain broke because Google decided the existing architecture enabled competitors to benefit from its curation work. The company acted unilaterally, with no opt-in period for tools to adjust.

## Why Position Numbers No Longer Work

Traditional rank tracking assumes a simple model: query → SERP → position → metric. That model is obsolete in modern search.

AI answer engines work differently. They fan out a single user query into multiple internal queries that never appear on a visible SERP. They synthesize results without showing a list of ten ranked links. They cite sources in summarized text, not ranked order. A brand can be cited prominently in an AI answer while a ranking tool shows no presence at all—or worse, shows a drop from a previous position that no longer exists.

Our recommendation: stop treating position as a primary success metric. It no longer reflects user experience. A human user sees your brand in an AI summary or knowledge panel. A ranking tool reports nothing, or reports a number that has no bearing on visibility. The disconnect is complete.

This problem worsens with voice search, image search, and any future interface that doesn't render a traditional SERP. The concept of "rank" becomes meaningless when there is no ranked list to see.

## Two Metrics That Matter Now: Visibility and Citations

[According to the reporting](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/rank-tracking-broke-in-september-the-future-is-measured-by-brand-revenue/592783/), success should be measured across two dimensions:

**Brand visibility** means the frequency and breadth of your brand appearing on any search surface—traditional organic results, AI answers, knowledge panels, images, and emerging formats. It is a comprehensive metric, not a single position. Different tools will measure this differently, so our recommendation is to accept that no single tracker will capture everything. You need a multi-tool approach that treats each surface separately and then aggregates them into a visibility narrative.

**AI citations** occur when an AI model explicitly names your brand or product as a source, usually with a URL. This is a direct signal of topical authority and builds user trust in ways that ranking alone does not. A single citation in a high-traffic AI answer can carry more value than ten organic position-one rankings in low-intent queries.

Teams that optimize for both serving (visibility) and citing (source attribution) dominate the modern SERP landscape. This is not a vanity metric play. Citations build authority. Visibility across surfaces protects you from volatility in any single feature.

## Building Resilient Reporting to Survive Blackouts

Outages will happen again. Google will change HTML structure. Third-party tools will fail. Our recommendation is to design your reporting around resilience, not perfection.

Build your success measurement into three layers:

- Aggregate visibility metrics from multiple independent sources rather than relying on one tool
- Track citations explicitly in AI answers and search summaries, separate from organic rankings
- Connect all metrics to revenue and customer behavior, not position numbers
- Document which data points go dark and when, so you can spot patterns and warn stakeholders early
- Automate alerts so you know within hours, not weeks, when a major tracker fails

The boardroom doesn't panic when a ranking tool goes offline if your CRM shows consistent pipeline growth. Revenue is the metric that survives every data blackout. Technical teams that measure real user behavior and tie it to actual business outcomes will survive the coming months of additional failures.

## What Is Still Unknown

We cannot yet verify how individual AI answer engines weight citations or how they decide which sources to include in synthesized results. This hypothesis needs testing: do multiple citations of your brand increase the likelihood of inclusion, or does one high-quality citation suffice? Do citations from different query types stack, or is each AI system independent?

We also cannot yet verify whether tracking tools will adapt faster than Google can close new data pathways. A hypothesis: third-party tools may shift to alternative data collection methods—user-reported visibility, crowdsourced SERP screenshots, API access where Google allows it. These would be slower and less standardized than current scraping-based systems, but might prove more resilient.

Finally, it remains unclear whether Google's actions will extend to blocking AI model crawlers from the search results page entirely. Stated as a hypothesis: if Google restricts AI crawler access to SERPs, it would fundamentally change how AI models train and respond, which would reshape the entire visibility landscape we're now learning to measure.
