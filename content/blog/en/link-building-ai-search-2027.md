Yes, link building still serves a purpose in AI search—but not the one most marketers learned. [According to Search Engine Journal reporting on Michael Johnson's upcoming webinar](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/link-building-strategies-for-2027-what-to-keep-and-cut/592119/), links remain a signal of relevance and a mechanism for page discovery in Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode. Google's own AI search guidance confirms that foundational SEO practices, including earned links, support both traditional rankings and AI visibility. The difference is tactical. Your link-building program needs to connect earned coverage to the specific questions your brand answers, not generic traffic goals.

## Links still signal relevance to AI systems

Google uses links for two things: discovering pages and evaluating their relevance. Both functions matter in AI search. [Search Engine Journal reports](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/link-building-strategies-for-2027-what-to-keep-and-cut/592119/) that when a relevant publication links to your resource, it gives readers a direct path to your expertise and gives Google another mechanism to discover that your brand has authority in that topic. A backlink does not guarantee a citation in an AI answer, but it remains a discovery and authority signal.

This aligns with what we see empirically. [Ahrefs found in its March 2026 citation study](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/link-building-strategies-for-2027-what-to-keep-and-cut/592119/) that 37.1% of URLs cited in AI Overviews also ranked in the top 10 organic results for the same query. Traditional search visibility has not disappeared; it has become one component of a broader visibility measurement. Our recommendation: continue to track both metrics separately and treat strong organic rankings as a foundation for AI citation, not a replacement for it.

## Your website is now AI's reference point for accuracy

The shift from clicks to citations changes what links need to support. [According to Newfold Digital's Sachin Puri in conversation with Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-search-is-changing-the-websites-job-not-replacing-it/592134/), your website serves as the authoritative hub for business information, even when customers discover you through an AI assistant. Links point to that hub. What matters is that the information inside it is accurate, consistent, and structured so AI systems can extract and act on it.

Puri illustrated this with a concrete example: an AI agent searching for an electrician available tomorrow afternoon, charging under $300, with strong reviews. The customer might never visit the electrician's website, but the agent still has to answer the same questions it would about any business. It needs service area, licensing, pricing, hours, and availability. If your website contradicts your Google Business Profile, or if schema markup shows an outdated price, AI systems now detect that inconsistency and downweight your relevance.

Our recommendation: treat your website structure, [schema markup](/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-answers), and factual consistency as prerequisites for link-building success. Links drive discovery, but accurate, structured information inside those links is what turns discovery into recommendations.

## Measure AI visibility separately from clicks

[Puri cited a 90-day Bluehost analysis](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-search-is-changing-the-websites-job-not-replacing-it/592134/) showing 83.4 million verified AI crawler requests per day across Bluehost-hosted sites. The breakdown was roughly 90% for model training, 9% for search indexing, and 1% for live assistant fetching. A hypothesis we cannot yet verify with complete confidence: this ratio varies widely by industry and topic authority, so your own site may see a different distribution.

More important than raw crawler volume: Bluehost's data suggested one observable referral visit for every 5,350 verified HTML crawl requests for ChatGPT and OpenAI. This is not a conversion rate. It reflects the fact that many AI-driven decisions happen somewhere else in the customer journey and never appear in your web analytics. Your link-building return on investment now has invisible components.

Our recommendation: build a measurement framework with three parts: visibility (is your site crawled and cited?), accuracy (are the answers about your business correct?), and outcomes (does the citation or recommendation lead to a booking, call, or action?). [Use a free audit](/free-audit) to check whether your site is even visible to AI crawlers before optimizing further.

## What to keep and cut from your link-building strategy

[Search Engine Journal's reporting on the upcoming webinar](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/link-building-strategies-for-2027-what-to-keep-and-cut/592119/) frames the decision as a "keep or cut" list. Tactics to retire include:

- Anchor text optimization for keyword ranking (AI systems evaluate topical relevance differently)
- Generic guest posts unrelated to your specific expertise
- Links from high-authority sites if the content context does not match your business
- Reciprocal linking programs focused on link volume alone

Tactics worth expanding:

- Links that point to original research, proprietary data, or tools your brand uniquely offers
- Coverage from publications your target audience already reads
- Links from businesses and experts in adjacent fields where you solve a specific problem
- Press coverage tied to newsworthy announcements or milestones

The underlying principle: relevant, earned links work. Irrelevant high-authority links do not—and AI systems may be better at detecting relevance than traditional ranking algorithms are.

## Align link acquisition to your core questions

Puri's example of a restaurant highlights the specificity AI systems now demand. A customer could ask for a family-owned, family-friendly restaurant with vegetarian and vegan options, wheelchair access, and an accessible restroom that can seat six people tonight. An AI agent cannot fill that request from general descriptions. It needs each attribute confirmed and availability verified.

This changes what you promote through links. A service page heavy on keywords and light on facts—"reliable electrical services"—gives an AI system almost nothing to work with. A page listing the services offered, service area, hours, licensing, and recent customer reviews gives the agent enough to match the business to a request and act on it.

Our recommendation: when you pursue earned links, promote content that answers a specific buyer question, not content designed for ranking keywords. That could be a detailed guide, a cost calculator, a tool that solves a particular problem, or original research your audience cares about. The link then becomes a referral from a trusted source to a resource that directly serves your audience—which is what both AI systems and human readers want.

## Put this into practice

Use the following checklist to audit your 2027 link-building plan:

- Identify the three to five core questions your brand uniquely answers
- Audit your existing backlinks and categorize each by topical relevance to those questions
- Retire outreach for links from high-authority sites if the audience or topic is unrelated
- [Check your schema markup and structured data for accuracy](/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-answers) before promoting any content for links
- Identify original assets (research, tools, guides) that answer buyer questions more specifically than competitors do
- Pitch those assets to publications your audience already reads, not just high-DA sites
- Set up tracking for AI citations separately from ranking positions and organic traffic
- Cross-check what AI systems say about your business against your website to catch inconsistencies
- Measure outcomes (bookings, calls, conversions) separately from pageviews

The goal of link building has not changed—earn trust and signal authority. The context has shifted. You are now signaling authority to AI systems that will recommend or describe your business to potential customers who may never click through to your site. [Structure your content so those systems can actually use what they find](/blog/structure-content-for-ai-answers), and build links that point to the proof points those systems need to recommend you.
