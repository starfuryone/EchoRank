## Le Monde sees no audience drop since AI Overviews launched—but the nuance matters

[According to Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/le-monde-says-ai-overviews-havent-hurt-its-audience-so-far/589018/), Le Monde reports that its total audience has not declined since Google launched AI Overviews in France. The French news publisher's CEO, Louis Dreyfus, addressed this seven weeks after the feature's launch, offering a counterpoint to earlier warnings from the French press alliance APIG that predicted traffic losses of 33% to 38%. However, Dreyfus clarified a critical distinction: APIG's figures measure only Google Search traffic, not total audience. Since search engines drive roughly half of visits to major news websites, a 30% reduction in search traffic would translate to a much smaller overall decline. Le Monde also welcomed record subscriber numbers—24,000 new subscribers in July and another 24,000 in August—though the interview did not tie these directly to any specific traffic source.

## The difference between search referrals and total audience is everything

APIG's 33-38% loss estimate applies to Google Search traffic only, not the total audience that reaches a website through all channels. This distinction shapes how you should interpret any publisher's claim about AI Overviews' impact. If search represents half of a site's traffic and search traffic falls 35%, the overall audience decline would be closer to 17.5%. Dreyfus emphasized this math explicitly in his interview, suggesting that many early fears conflated two different metrics. Le Monde's apps generated 36% of the group's total traffic in August, meaning the company relies on non-web channels more heavily than some competitors. The publisher also noted that Le Monde in English, available in markets where AI Overviews were already live, grew by 7% in the first half of 2026 versus the same period in 2025.

## News content behaves differently from service content under AI Overviews

Dreyfus distinguished between news and service content when discussing AI Overviews' effects. News articles appear to have remained relatively stable, whereas service-oriented sectors such as health, food, and automotive may have seen audience declines of up to 50%. This observation is based on his accounts of conversations with other publishers, not published data. The hypothesis that news content performs differently from service content makes intuitive sense: an AI response to a breaking news story might drive readers toward the full article for context and analysis, while an AI answer to a health question or product recommendation might satisfy the searcher's immediate need. Our recommendation is to audit your own content by category to identify which of your sections face the most exposure risk under AI Overviews.

## The industry still cannot measure AI Overviews' impact consistently

Florent Rimbert, who oversees digital development and advertising at APIG, told Minted that the alliance has no combined figures yet. Jean-Paul Dietsch, general director of the audience certifier ACPM, said he saw no collapse in certified traffic in August but noted that ACPM currently has no way to tie visits to a given Google feature. A separate [University of Washington working paper](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/what-wikipedia-reveals-about-ai-overviews-and-web-traffic/589042/) estimates that AI Overviews reduced monthly search referrals to English Wikipedia by roughly 5% after the feature became the U.S. default in 2024, though this figure is subject to methodological limitations. The measurement problem cuts both ways: publishers cannot yet isolate whether traffic drops are due to AI Overviews specifically or to other 2024 events, and they lack standardized tools to track which content appears in AI responses.

## Le Monde's specific advantages may not apply to other publishers

Le Monde's experience offers partial insight into AI Overviews' effects, but the company's advantages are worth naming. Its subscription model converts referrals into direct revenue, reducing reliance on ad-supported web traffic. Its apps represent a significant share of total traffic, insulating the company from search-driven fluctuations. The company also has content agreements with OpenAI (March 2024), Perplexity (May 2025), and Meta (December 2025), and Dreyfus noted that ChatGPT referrals convert to subscribers at rates 26 times higher than Facebook and 260 times higher than Discover. This last figure shows relative conversion rates rather than actual traffic numbers. A publisher that depends entirely on search-driven ad revenue would face a very different calculus. We cannot yet verify whether Le Mondo's experience generalizes to publishers without direct AI content agreements or subscription models.

## What remains unknown: the long-term pattern

[Search Engine Journal notes](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/what-wikipedia-reveals-about-ai-overviews-and-web-traffic/589042/) that the industry is still early in measuring how AI Overviews affect referral traffic. The Wikimedia Foundation's October 2025 data showed a decline of about 8% in overall human pageviews across all Wikipedia languages compared to the same period in 2024, attributed to generative AI and social media platforms—but this is not a direct causal estimate of AI Overviews alone. The University of Washington paper's earlier version reported a 15% decline, later revised downward to 5.45% when the authors switched to monthly search referrals and added more comparison groups. These revisions underscore how sensitive impact estimates are to methodological choices.

A hypothesis worth tracking: as more publishers report their own data in coming months, we may find that the initial APIG warning of 33-38% losses applies narrowly to certain sectors or search types, while others experience minimal impact. The industry may not converge on a single number because different content types and business models respond to AI Overviews very differently.

## Practical steps for publishers watching this unfold

- Segment your traffic by content type (news, service, product guides) to identify which sections are most exposed.
- Establish baseline metrics for how often your content appears in AI Overviews before trying to measure change.
- Compare your search referrals against non-search channels to understand how much AI-driven loss would actually affect total audience.
- Review content agreements with AI companies to ensure you have visibility into how and where your content is being used.
- Monitor which of your articles are referenced in AI responses versus buried below the fold.
