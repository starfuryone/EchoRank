## Argon is now available to paid AI Mode users, but only if they opt in

Google added Gemini 4 Argon to AI Mode's model menu for Pro and Ultra subscribers, [announced by Robby Stein, VP of Product for Google Search, on X](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-gemini-4-argon-ai-mode/592806/). Users can switch to Argon from the same "+" icon that previously added Gemini 3.7 Flash and 3.8 Flash. Unlike those Flash models, Argon is not the default—it remains optional. The rollout began alongside Argon's availability to developers, enterprises, and Gemini app users on the same day.

## Argon handles multi-step reasoning and follow-up conversations better than previous models

Google claims Argon brings improvements to multi-step tasks and back-and-forth conversations, with better instruction-following and style compliance than earlier Gemini versions. The model was introduced September 30 as a frontier model for coding, enterprise work, and cyber defense. In AI Mode specifically, the focus is on nuanced reasoning and conversational depth—the kinds of tasks where users ask follow-up questions and expect the model to track context.

A hypothesis: the model's strength in multi-turn conversations could make it attractive to users asking research questions or refining their inquiries. If enough Pro and Ultra subscribers switch to Argon, it could shift which sources AI Mode citations favor—not because Argon ranks sources differently internally, but because more queries run through it. However, we cannot yet verify whether Argon's retrieval or weighting behavior differs from earlier models, or whether Google has tuned it differently for Search.

## The bigger question: will Argon stay optional or become the default?

[Search Engine Journal notes that Gemini 3 Flash and 3.5 Flash both launched as defaults, while Gemini 3 Pro launched as an option and remained one](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-gemini-4-argon-ai-mode/592806/). Argon has so far followed the Pro path—subscriber-only, not mandatory. Google's announcement does not clarify which direction Argon will take, and the company has hinted at more Gemini 4 models coming to AI Mode without specifying which surfaces they will reach.

Our recommendation: monitor your AI Mode citation traffic over the next four weeks. If you use [our free audit](/free-audit), track whether your impressions in generative AI features shift as Argon adoption grows. Watch for patterns in which queries route to Argon users and whether those queries pull from your content differently than they did before.

## Why this matters for your citations

Your citation risk under Argon depends on two unknowns. First, how many users will opt into it. Second, whether Google will eventually make it the default for all AI Mode users, free or paid.

If Argon stays optional, the citation impact will be gradual and weighted toward paid users—a smaller but often higher-intent audience. If it becomes the default, the impact becomes immediate and universal. Neither Google nor the reporting specifies Argon's retrieval behavior or how it weights sources compared to Flash models.

A hypothesis: frontier models like Argon are typically built for complexity and reasoning depth, not for speed or cost-efficiency. Google's [reasoning for running Flash models at Search scale is that they cost less and respond faster](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-gemini-4-argon-ai-mode/592806/). Making Argon the default would require either accepting higher costs at Search scale or assuming that users value better reasoning enough to justify slower responses. We cannot yet verify whether Google intends either path.

## How to test Argon's impact on your content

The best test is direct: run the same multi-step question through both 3.8 Flash and Argon, then note which sources each model cites. A multi-step task with follow-up questions is the ideal scenario because that is where Google claims Argon excels.

For example, ask both models to explain a process in your industry, then ask a follow-up that requires the model to refine or contextualize its first answer. Compare the sources each model cited. If Argon consistently favors different sources, that pattern matters for your strategy. If it cites the same sources in the same order, the difference may be narrower than Google's claims suggest.

Keep notes on:

- Which model answers more of your multi-turn query
- Whether each model cites your site or your competitors
- Whether the same sources appear in both model responses
- Whether Argon asks clarifying questions or refines its reasoning more often than Flash

This is not a substitute for monitoring your actual AI Mode impression data, but it provides a faster signal of whether Argon's reasoning depth translates into different source selection.

## Language support and rollout timeline remain unclear

Google has not announced which languages Argon supports in AI Mode. [The Gemini 3.7 Flash rollout was English-only initially](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-gemini-4-argon-ai-mode/592806/), suggesting Argon may follow the same pattern. If you serve markets outside the United States or operate in non-English languages, Argon may not affect your visibility immediately—or at all, depending on Google's language roadmap.

The rollout is ongoing. As of the time of the original reporting, Argon had not appeared in all Pro accounts yet, which means not all eligible users have access. Wait until the rollout completes before drawing conclusions from early citation data.
