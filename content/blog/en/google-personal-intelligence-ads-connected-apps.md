## Google connected apps now shape AI Mode ads, but marketers have no new controls

[Google updated its privacy policy effective October 16](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-privacy-policy-connected-apps-ai-mode-ads/594546/) to allow Gmail, Google Photos, and Google Drive content to influence personalized ads when users connect those services to Search or Gemini. The revision applies to searchers who opt into Personal Intelligence, a feature that lets AI Mode pull context from connected apps. Google is currently testing these ads with eligible campaigns. Marketers cannot directly target or bid on connected app signals, and no new reporting or audience controls have been announced.

## How connected app data reaches ad personalization

Google's updated policy keeps its longstanding statement that it does not show personalized ads based on Drive, Gmail, or Photos content. [The exception added states](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-privacy-policy-connected-apps-ai-mode-ads/594546/) that users can make that data available to other Google services that support personalized ads. Search may use summaries, excerpts, and inferences from connected sources to personalize content and ads for users who enable the connection.

Personal Intelligence is an opt-in feature that launched in January 2026 for Google AI Pro and AI Ultra subscribers in the U.S. When enabled, AI Mode can reference a hotel booking or flight confirmation in Gmail alongside travel pictures in Photos to tailor responses. Google is now testing ads within that connected experience.

The mechanics are indirect. Ads are not matched to raw email or photo content. Instead, [Google processes the information into summaries, excerpts, and inferences](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-privacy-policy-connected-apps-ai-mode-ads/594546/), and those derived signals may inform which ads appear. A shopper who has disabled ad personalization in My Ad Center cannot stop connected apps from feeding data into AI features—disconnecting the apps is the only way to remove that content entirely.

## What marketers can and cannot do

If you manage Google Ads campaigns, you will not find new targeting toggles or audience segments for connected app insights. [Campaign eligibility follows existing rules for ads in AI Mode](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-privacy-policy-connected-apps-ai-mode-ads/594546/): Performance Max, Shopping, and Search campaigns using broad match, including AI Max for Search.

Our recommendation is to treat this as a signal you cannot directly influence or measure. Connected app inferences can affect which ads an opted-in user sees, but you cannot target by Gmail insights, Photos-based behavior, or Drive history. Google has not announced dedicated reporting, placement controls, or separate eligibility requirements for this test.

The available data on campaign performance remains the same. You can observe whether a campaign serves impressions and conversions, but you cannot isolate the contribution of connected app signals. This creates a gap between what shapes the ad and what you can measure.

### Eligibility and campaign setup

- Performance Max campaigns
- Shopping campaigns
- Search campaigns using broad match or AI Max for Search
- No new setup required; connected app signals work alongside existing Smart Bidding rules
- No advertiser dashboard controls to enable or disable the feature

## Questions that remain unanswered

A hypothesis we cannot yet verify is whether connected-app signals personalize ads only within AI Mode or across broader Google Search Services. [Google's help documentation phrases the question for "Search Services" as a whole](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-privacy-policy-connected-apps-ai-mode-ads/594546/), which includes Google Search (including AI Mode and Discover), Maps, Shopping, Flights, Hotels, Translate, and News. The scope of the test's reach is unclear.

Google has also not stated when AI Mode ads for users with Personal Intelligence enabled will exit testing or whether advertisers will eventually receive dedicated reporting or placement controls. The current test runs indefinitely without a published roadmap.

A hypothesis we cannot verify is how Google weights connected app signals relative to other personalization inputs like search history, location, and device type. No internal ranking or weighting mechanism has been disclosed.

## Why this matters for your AI visibility strategy

This update shows how Google is layering personal data into AI answer personalization. Connected apps create a more complete user context, which Google can reference when generating responses and selecting ads. [As we discuss in our guide to structuring content for AI answers](/blog/structure-content-for-ai-answers), the way AI systems retrieve and rank information differs from search rankings. When Google incorporates inferred data from connected apps, the route by which your content reaches an answer shifts.

If your product or service fits a use case that connected app data reveals—travel planning, shopping, accommodation booking—users with Personal Intelligence enabled may see ads that feel more contextually matched. That relevance could improve performance for brands that fit the pattern. For others, the same mechanism could cause ads to be shown to audiences less likely to convert, if the inferences misrepresent the user's intent.

Our recommendation is to monitor campaign performance trends in eligible campaign types and compare results over time. Watch for changes in impression volume or cost per conversion in Performance Max and broad-match Search campaigns serving U.S. traffic. Shifts in performance may reflect the test's expansion or changes in how connected app signals influence targeting, even if you cannot see the mechanism directly.

The broader implication is that personalization is moving beyond explicit signals. Connected apps represent an expansion of what Google considers relevant context.
