## AI Overviews have invaded branded searches—and they're growing fast

[Ahrefs found that AI Overviews now appear in 83% of branded search results](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-on-branded-searches/), up from 65% in July 2026. The real problem: in 76% of those cases, the AI Overview sits in position one, displacing your domain from the top ranking. Most citations come from Wikipedia, YouTube, and LinkedIn—sources you cannot control. This represents a fundamental shift in how search results present your brand to the public, and it demands immediate attention from anyone responsible for brand visibility.

## Why branded searches matter most for your visibility

Branded searches are the highest-intent queries your audience makes. Someone typing your brand name or exact product already knows who you are and wants to reach you directly. Unlike unbranded searches, where you compete for attention, branded searches should be yours to own. The problem with AI Overviews is that they intercept that intent before the user clicks through to your site.

[Ahrefs analyzed 232.8 million US desktop search crawls from July to September 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-on-branded-searches/). The data is stark: AI Overviews appeared in 82.91% of branded keyword results as of September 29th, with monthly averages rising from 61.3% in July to 73.3% in September—a 12-percentage-point jump in three months. Non-branded searches also saw AI Overview growth, but the increase was smaller, meaning Google is aggressively pushing these summaries precisely where brands expect to own the results.

## Position one is now owned by Google's summary, not your domain

The most damaging finding: [when Ahrefs tracked 100 major brand names at weekly intervals, 63 had AI Overviews by September 30th, and 48 of those (76.2%) appeared in position one](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-on-branded-searches/). The average position was 1.49. This means your domain is being pushed down the SERP in its own branded query. Users see the AI-generated summary first, and many never scroll past it to reach your official website.

Our recommendation: treat this as a critical change to your search positioning. You can no longer assume that ranking first for your brand name equals owning the first search result impression. The AI Overview now mediates that impression.

## Third-party sources control the narrative

[Most citations in these AI Overviews pointed to Wikipedia, YouTube, and LinkedIn](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-on-branded-searches/)—platforms where brands have limited editorial control. Wikipedia cited in almost 72% of external references. YouTube and LinkedIn made up the remainder, both harder to influence than your own domain. This matters because the AI Overview shapes the public's understanding of your brand using sources you did not write and cannot easily correct.

The good news is narrow: [across 58 AI Overviews analyzed, only two explicitly mentioned competitors](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-on-branded-searches/), and only one competitor-owned website appeared in the source citations. Google's systems seem to avoid sourcing from rival brands. But the absence of named competitors does not solve the real problem—your brand's story is being told by Wikipedia, not by you.

A hypothesis worth testing: as AI Overviews become more widespread, brands will face pressure to increase presence on Wikipedia and YouTube, not because these are ideal marketing channels, but because they are now primary shapers of search result impression.

## What we still cannot verify

Google has not published guidelines for how branded AI Overviews are ranked or weighted, and we cannot yet verify whether [the gap between branded and non-branded AI Overview triggers](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-on-branded-searches/) will continue to narrow or stabilize. The data shows the gap narrowed from 12.2 percentage points in July to 5 points in September, but stated as a hypothesis, we do not know if Google intends to reach parity or if seasonal factors or query volume drove the change.

It is also unknown whether Google will move AI Overviews lower in the SERP or add user controls to suppress them. The current trend suggests the opposite—Google appears committed to inserting these summaries into any result that will tolerate them—but search results change frequently and unpredictably.

## What to do right now

- Audit your branded keywords to see which ones trigger AI Overviews and where they appear
- Check which external sources (Wikipedia, YouTube, LinkedIn, etc.) are being cited for your brand
- Review the accuracy of those citations and flag any outdated or misleading information
- Ensure your official channels and properties are crawlable by AI systems and structured for easy extraction
- Monitor your branded search click-through rates to measure impact

## Check your brand's AI Overview exposure

To find which of your branded keywords trigger AI Overviews, [Ahrefs recommends using Site Explorer's Organic Keywords report, filtering by Branded Keywords and then by SERP Features set to AI Overviews](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-on-branded-searches/). This shows you exactly which branded queries now display the summary, giving you a baseline to track changes over time.

We recommend focusing first on your most critical branded keywords—your company name, key product names, and executive names if they appear in search. These are the queries that carry the highest business value and the most risk of misdirection.
