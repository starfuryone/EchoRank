AI Overviews have spread to most searches for major global brands. [Ahrefs analyzed exact brand-name queries for 100 leading global brands from July through September and found that 63 of those 100 brand-name searches displayed an AI Overview by September 30](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-big-brand-searches/592280/). This is the results page where people search your company's name—your primary branded real estate on Google—and it now carries answers citing external websites instead of leading directly to your own.

Two other separate analyses published the same week corroborate the trend. [Chris Long, co-founder of Nectiv, reported 93 out of 100 enterprise brands had AI Overviews present](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-big-brand-searches/592280/). [DemandSphere's platform showed AI Overviews reaching 90.48% of branded keywords by September 27](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-big-brand-searches/592280/), though the percentage dipped slightly to 82.06% by September 29. The consistency across these three independent datasets points to a real and rapid shift in how Google handles brand queries.

For brand and marketing teams, this represents a significant change in search behavior. Your brand's name—the query you own—now competes with a synthesized answer. That answer cites external sources, can reference competitors, and positions itself above your organic listing.

## AI Overviews Appeared on Most Brand Queries Almost Overnight

AI Overviews grew from rare to routine on branded searches between early July and late September. The acceleration was sharp. [Ahrefs tracked their panel of 100 brands weekly: five had AI Overviews on July 1, but the count jumped to 42 on September 9, then climbed to 63 by September 30](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-big-brand-searches/592280/). The fastest growth happened in early September.

A hypothesis: Google likely launched AI Overviews on branded keywords as a feature, then refined visibility and response logic during this period. The data from multiple vendors shows the trend clearly, but none of the reports explain why September 9 was a significant inflection point.

Our recommendation is to check your own brand-name SERP now. Search your company's exact name on Google U.S. desktop. Does an AI Overview appear? If yes, note its position and which external sites it cites. If no, expect one to appear soon based on these findings.

## External Sources Are Cited in Brand-Name Answers

The placement of an AI Overview on your brand's primary search result means external websites gain visibility in your owned search space. [Ahrefs analyzed 57 brand-name AI Overviews collected between September 16 and 29 and recorded which outside domains were cited in the answers](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-big-brand-searches/592280/). The most frequently cited domains were Wikipedia, YouTube, LinkedIn, and news sites.

One notable finding: [out of 57 source lists, Ahrefs identified just one verified competitor-owned website](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-big-brand-searches/592280/). But this does not mean competitors are absent from the answers. [Among 58 responses where the full text was accessible, two (or 3.4%) directly discussed competitors—for example, Pepsi's answer referenced Coca-Cola during discussion of the "cola wars," and Intel's response mentioned TSMC and Nvidia](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-big-brand-searches/592280/).

We cannot yet verify how Google decides which external domains to cite or how much competitor mentions influence brand perception in these answers. The data shows the pattern exists, but not the mechanism behind it.

A hypothesis: Google may cite Wikipedia and news sources because they tend to carry neutral, encyclopedic information. Competitor mentions appear only when directly relevant to answering the user's question, not as default behavior.

Our recommendation: examine your brand's AI Overview and the domains it cites. Use [Echorank's free audit](/free-audit) to see which external sources are competing for visibility in your brand space.

## Your Organic Listing Is Now Lower on the Page

When an AI Overview appears on your brand-name search, it occupies prime real estate. [Ahrefs recorded the position of each of the 63 AI Overviews on September 30: 48 appeared in position one, eight in position two, two in position three, three in position four, and two in position six](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-big-brand-searches/592280/). The average position was 1.49, meaning most AI Overviews sit at the very top of the results page.

This does not directly tell us where your brand's organic listing moved, but the implication is clear: the AI Overview pushed down everything below it. Users see the synthesized answer first. Your official brand page is no longer the first search result for your brand's name.

A hypothesis: Google may eventually lower AI Overviews on branded queries if user feedback suggests they prefer organic brand listings, as Ahrefs speculated. We cannot yet verify whether this will happen or how soon.

Our recommendation: track changes to your brand-name SERP over time. Take dated screenshots of the full results page, noting AI Overview presence and position, so you can document whether Google moves these answers lower or removes them. We recommend checking monthly.

## What Brand and SEO Teams Should Do Now

A practical checklist to monitor and respond to AI Overviews on your branded searches:

- Search your company's exact name on Google U.S. desktop and note whether an AI Overview appears.
- If an AI Overview exists, take a screenshot of the full results page, including the answer text and cited domains.
- Identify which external sources the AI Overview cites and verify whether any belong to competitors or unauthorized partners.
- Check whether your brand's official website still appears in the organic results below the AI Overview.
- Repeat this check weekly or monthly to track changes in AI Overview presence, position, or cited sources.
- Use Google Search Console's branded queries filter (available since March 2026) to monitor clicks and impressions on brand-name searches.
- Document cases where competitor information appears in your brand-name AI Overview and consider reporting them to Google.

The data from [Ahrefs, Chris Long, and DemandSphere](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-big-brand-searches/592280/) all point to the same conclusion: AI Overviews are now the norm for branded searches, not an exception. The speed of this rollout—from 5% to 63% in three months—suggests Google sees this as a core feature for brand queries.

For brand teams, this shifts the game. Your brand-name search is no longer a guaranteed path to your website; it is now a gateway through an AI-mediated answer that may or may not credit your official sources. [Tracking which sites Google cites in these answers](/blog/track-brand-mentions-across-llms) and monitoring whether [your brand is mentioned but not quoted](/blog/find-your-ai-citation-gap) in AI Overviews are now essential practices.

The opportunity lies in ensuring Google has access to your authoritative brand information. [Make your site readable to AI crawlers](/blog/make-your-site-readable-to-ai-crawlers) so that when Google constructs an AI Overview for your brand name, it can cite your own pages as primary sources, not external intermediaries.
