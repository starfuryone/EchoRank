Most of the ChatGPT ads that Adthena counted in beauty and travel ran against research-style prompts, meaning queries where a person asks for comparisons or what is best rather than naming a product. Adthena's Holiday AI Paid Search Report, covered by [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ai-answer-ads-apparel-electronics-report/595984/), counted about 204,489 ChatGPT ad placements and about 175,100 placements inside Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode across five holiday categories in the U.S. from June to September. The numbers show where paid results are appearing. They do not show whether anyone clicked, what was spent, or what converted. That gap matters more than the headline counts.

## What the report counted

**Adthena's report, as summarized by Search Engine Journal, covers five categories: apparel, consumer electronics, travel, toys and games, and beauty.** Across Google's AI surfaces, apparel accounted for roughly 111,000 placements and consumer electronics about 55,000. Travel had roughly 6,500, beauty about 2,100, and toys and games around 685. On the ChatGPT side, the report gives apparel 90,517 placements, consumer electronics 56,569, travel 48,134, toys and games 4,694 and beauty 4,575.

The same coverage notes that the number of distinct advertisers in those categories on ChatGPT rose from 1,082 in June to 6,333 in August. Adthena also reported a steep rise in toys and games advertisers, from a low base. Treat these as observations from one vendor's sample. The report does not say which markets or queries it monitored across all four months, and it sells ad tracking for ChatGPT, so its framing has a commercial interest.

The Search Engine Journal piece also raises a point that many readers will miss: the report's ChatGPT figures come from a period when OpenAI was expanding ads into new regions. OpenAI's own October figure of more than 100,000 advertisers covers all categories across roughly eight months, so it cannot be compared directly with Adthena's monthly counts.

## Research prompts versus product prompts

**In beauty, 83% of observed ChatGPT ads ran on research and discovery queries; in travel it was 69%, and in toys and games 67%.** Apparel was at 48% and consumer electronics at 42%. Adthena defines research queries as "people comparing or asking what's best rather than naming a product." The report does not say how individual queries were sorted into that bucket, so the percentages describe Adthena's classification, not a verified measure of user intent.

Our recommendation is to read the split as a signal about where commercial answers are being placed, not as proof of a durable pattern. The categories where comparison questions dominate are the ones where a shopper is most likely to want a recommendation before naming a brand. If your category follows that pattern, the comparison and best-of content you publish is more likely to be the material an answer engine or an ad system draws on.

## What Google's AI placements mean for existing campaigns

**Google's help documentation, as reported in the same coverage, makes AI Overviews and AI Mode ads automatic for eligible Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns.** Advertisers cannot buy these placements separately or opt out of them apart from Search. Negative keywords and brand exclusions are the available controls.

That means some advertisers may already be serving in these surfaces without a deliberate decision. The report also does not separate AI placements from ordinary Search in standard reporting, so they can be hard to find in your search terms data. Our recommendation is to review which campaigns are eligible, pull search terms that look like research queries, and decide whether any need negative keyword changes. That is a bounded task and worth doing before the next budget review.

Google has also moved since the report's window closed. Coverage notes that multi-turn ads began rolling out in the U.S. on October 28, and that AI Mode ads were scaling to more countries. Adthena's data ends in September and does not capture those changes.

## Reddit and TikTok are moving in the same direction

**Two related items in the same news cycle point the same way.** Reddit has started testing ads in its own search results with a select group of advertisers, according to [Search Engine Journal's report on the test](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/reddit-begins-testing-ads-in-its-own-search-results/595935/). Reddit's post does not describe which advertisers were chosen, the markets, or the pricing model. A separate [Search Engine Journal piece on TikTok](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/tiktok-search-ads-are-becoming-real-search-advertising/596016/) describes TikTok's Search Ads Campaign, which uses keyword matching and reporting that paid search managers will recognise, while noting differences in how negative keywords handle misspellings and plurals.

Our recommendation is not to rebuild a strategy around any one of these. Each is in test or early rollout, and the metrics needed to judge them are not public. The useful observation is that paid placements are appearing wherever a person types a question, and that is the same place where organic answers are being generated.

## What is still unknown

- **Hypothesis:** the research-prompt share in beauty may reflect how beauty shoppers search, or it may reflect how Adthena sorted queries. We cannot yet verify which.
- **We cannot yet verify** whether AI placements drive clicks or conversions at a rate comparable to standard Search. The report does not measure either.
- **We cannot yet verify** how these placements interact with organic citation in AI answers. Paid and organic results may overlap, compete, or be unrelated, and no public data settles it.

## A short checklist for the next review

- Confirm which of your Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns are eligible for AI Overviews and AI Mode placements.
- Pull search terms and flag the ones that read as comparisons or "what is best" questions.
- Decide whether any of those terms need negative keywords or brand exclusions, and document the reason.
- List the comparison and best-of questions your category gets asked, and check whether your site answers them directly.
- Track how often your brand is mentioned in AI answers over time, not just in a single snapshot.
