Three separate changes landed in the same week, and the most useful thing a site owner can do right now is keep them apart. Google started rolling out its November 2026 core update on Nov. 10, according to [Search Engine Journal's SEO Pulse roundup](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/seo-pulse-core-update-chatgpt-business-hub-ai-modes-new-model/597135/). Two days later Google also made Gemini 4 Hydrogen the default model in AI Mode, and OpenAI opened Business Hub in beta on Nov. 10. Each of these can move the numbers you see in your reports, which means that reading them as one event will mislead you.

Our recommendation is simple: write down all three dates in your tracking notes before you look at any traffic or citation chart this month.

## What happened this week

The short version, as reported by Search Engine Journal, is that Google's update is the third Search core update of the year and applies globally and to all languages. Google's Search Status Dashboard says it may take up to two weeks to finish. The Pulse piece notes it was still in progress on Nov. 12, so the end date is not known yet.

OpenAI's Business Hub lets businesses claim a ChatGPT profile and see aggregated mentions, website clicks and discovery topics for the previous 28 days. A mention, according to OpenAI's setup guide as described in [Search Engine Journal's submission walkthrough](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/submit-website-chatgpt/596785/), counts when ChatGPT references your website. The brand name alone does not count. Vahan Petrosyan, SEJ's director of technology, reported 353,356 mentions and 2,663 website clicks for Search Engine Journal over Oct. 14 to Nov. 10. That is one publisher's report, not a benchmark, and the walkthrough itself warns that a blank metric does not always mean zero activity.

On Nov. 12, Google made Gemini 4 Hydrogen the default model in AI Mode for all users worldwide, as [Search Engine Journal reported](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-gemini-4-hydrogen-default-ai-mode/597096/). Robby Stein, VP of Product for Google Search, said on X that Hydrogen is also handling the hardest questions in AI Overviews. Google's own post says the model improves query fan-out and connects users to a broader range of helpful links. Neither Google nor Stein gave link counts, and neither said which questions count as hardest.

Earnings calls from the same period also turned up search referral declines. Ziff Davis's CEO said the company is "not planning on a search recovery," and organic Google referrals to its properties fell about 37% year over year in the quarter. Tripadvisor took a $369.5 million non-cash goodwill impairment and said SEO will still be a headwind in 2027. Those are company-level figures from their own reports, and they describe their portfolios, not yours.

## What it means for AI visibility

Our recommendation is to treat the November period as three experiments that overlap, not one.

For the core update, Google's own guidance is to wait at least a full week after rollout and compare that week in Search Console with the week before rollout began. Going by the two-week estimate, the first full post-rollout week falls across U.S. Thanksgiving and Black Friday and Cyber Monday. Retailers will be comparing holiday traffic against an ordinary week. Build that into any comparison you publish.

For Business Hub, our recommendation is to use the click counts as a cross-check, not a replacement. OpenAI describes a recorded click as something other than a confirmed visit, so set the 28-day click figure beside the ChatGPT referrals already in your analytics. Claiming the profile is worth doing if you are eligible, but OpenAI is clear that claiming does not guarantee higher placement, so do not treat it as a visibility lever on its own.

For AI Mode and AI Overviews, log the Nov. 12 model change as its own line in your citation tracking. Any change in how often you are cited after that date could come from the model, the core update, or both. You can check Google's claim of a broader set of links by looking at your own citation and referral counts. Do not expect a number from Google to settle it.

Our recommendation for the pulse-check cadence is a short written log:

- Record the core update start date (Nov. 10) and the date you confirm it has finished on the Search Status Dashboard.
- Record the Business Hub claim date and the first 28-day window you pull.
- Record the Nov. 12 AI Mode default change, separately from the core update.
- Pull Search Console comparisons only after the post-rollout week has closed.
- Annotate every chart with those dates before sharing it with anyone.

## What is still unknown

Several things here are not settled, and we cannot yet verify them.

It is a hypothesis that Hydrogen will change which sites get cited in AI Overviews, because Google has not said which questions go to it. Stein's thread names the hardest questions without defining them, so until Google publishes a clearer rule, any change you see is hard to attribute.

It is also a hypothesis that the core update will reshuffle AI citations. Core updates target ranking in organic results, and whether that feeds through to AI answer citations is not something the sources establish. We cannot verify the link between the two from this reporting.

We also cannot yet say how long the core update will run. The dashboard gives an estimate of up to two weeks, and the Pulse article confirms only that it was still rolling out on Nov. 12.

Finally, the Business Hub numbers from one publisher are not a baseline for anyone else. Mention and click counts depend on how often ChatGPT references your site, and OpenAI's reporting uses thresholds, so small sites may see blanks that do not mean no activity.

## A checklist before you draw conclusions

- Confirm the rollout is finished on the Search Status Dashboard before you call a winner or loser.
- Use a full post-rollout week, not a partial one, for Search Console comparisons.
- Pull the Business Hub 28-day window and set clicks next to your analytics referrals.
- Log the Nov. 12 AI Mode change separately from the core update.
- Mark holiday weeks in any retail comparison.
- Hold off on attributing a citation shift to a single cause until you have at least one clean window on each side of each date.
