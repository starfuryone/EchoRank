## Direct checkout is now live for Canadian shoppers in AI Mode

[Google has launched direct checkout powered by the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) to shoppers in Canada](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-brings-ucp-direct-checkout-to-ai-mode-in-canada/595882/) across AI Mode in Google Search and the Gemini app. The rollout, announced on November 4, 2026, allows eligible retailers to let customers complete purchases without leaving the conversation. Shoppers pay through Google Pay using saved wallet details. The initial launch includes four retail partners and merchant groups, though Google has not disclosed which retailers are live. Broader access is tied to a Merchant Center update arriving next year, and many details about reporting and attribution remain unknown.

## What the checkout experience looks like for shoppers

Shoppers researching products in AI Mode or Gemini can now complete transactions directly within Google's surfaces. When a purchase is made, Google Pay handles the payment using existing wallet information and shipping details. Retailers remain the seller of record, meaning they control the fulfillment and customer relationship. Google notes that in coming months, retailers will gain the ability to add loyalty rewards and other capabilities to the checkout flow. The transaction stays within Google's interface, keeping friction low while ensuring merchants retain full control over their operations.

When UCP integration opens to more retailers, merchants with clean, detailed feeds will be positioned to adopt the feature faster and avoid delays.

## The Merchant Center roadmap for 2027

Starting next year, Google will offer simplified UCP integration directly inside Merchant Center. This update will let retailers enable direct checkout for their product listings across AI Mode and Gemini without building custom integrations. However, Google has not yet shared a specific release date, eligibility criteria, or onboarding steps for retailers outside the initial launch group. The company also hasn't disclosed how direct checkout orders will be reported in Merchant Center or Google Ads, or how these transactions interact with paid Shopping and Performance Max campaigns. A hypothesis is that Google will provide attribution similar to organic search results, but this remains unconfirmed.

To stay ahead of the Merchant Center rollout, focus on these steps:

## What we still don't know

Several critical details remain unclear. [Google hasn't specified which retailers are included in the initial launch or disclosed eligibility criteria for broader access](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-brings-ucp-direct-checkout-to-ai-mode-in-canada/595882/). It's also unknown how UCP direct checkout will be tracked and attributed in Merchant Center and Google Ads. A hypothesis is that Google will provide separate reporting for direct checkout transactions, but vendors have not confirmed this. The interaction between direct checkout and existing paid campaigns—Shopping Ads and Performance Max—is also unstated as a hypothesis. Finally, we cannot yet verify whether loyalty programs and other post-purchase capabilities will be available to all merchants or only launch partners.

## Parallel expansion: home services booking in AI Mode

In a related announcement, [Google is also adding home service booking and quote request links to AI Mode through partner platforms such as Jobber, Housecall Pro, Angi, and Thumbtack](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ai-mode-home-services-booking-quote-links/595871/). This feature is live in the U.S. Homeowners can request bookings directly from AI Mode, and those requests flow into the service provider's platform with no lead fees from Jobber or Housecall Pro. This expansion shows Google's broader push to enable commerce and booking directly within AI surfaces. While this rollout is separate from UCP direct checkout, it underscores the same strategy: reducing friction by letting customers complete actions without leaving the conversation.

## The bigger picture: UCP as an open standard

Google launched UCP earlier this year alongside industry partners as an open standard for agentic commerce. The protocol creates a shared language for AI agents, consumer surfaces, businesses, and payment providers to work together across product discovery, purchasing, and post-purchase support. [Shopify VP Mani Fazeli noted that Shopify merchants selling into Canada can now be faithfully represented and bought from directly in AI Mode and Gemini with zero extra work](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-brings-ucp-direct-checkout-to-ai-mode-in-canada/595882/), underscoring that the protocol is designed to lower the barrier to entry. However, the initial Canadian rollout applies only to select Shopify merchants, not all of them. This suggests that even with simplified integration coming next year, merchants will still need to meet qualification criteria.
