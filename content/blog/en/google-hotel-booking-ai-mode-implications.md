## Hotel booking in AI Mode is now live—but unanswered questions remain about how it ranks you

[According to Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-starts-rolling-out-hotel-booking-in-ai-mode/587288/), Google has started rolling out hotel booking through AI Mode in Search for U.S. users in English. Users can describe a trip and preferences, receive a visual list of hotel options with guest reviews, and complete the reservation without leaving Google through a "Continue on Google" flow powered by Google Pay. Initial partners include Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Priceline, and Trip.com for online travel agencies, plus Choice Hotels International, Hilton, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Marriott International, and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts as hotel chains. Flight price alerts and points-or-miles display have also gone live, but you still cannot complete a flight purchase inside AI Mode—that remains on the partner site.

This is a structural shift in how transactional searches work. For years, users landed on partner sites to buy. Now, for hotels at least, the transaction lives inside the AI response. The implications for visibility are immediate and complicated.

## What changed: transactions now happen inside the AI, not on your site

Google previously told the industry in November 2025 that users would eventually finish bookings inside AI Mode. That was a statement of intent. Now it is shipping. The hotel feature is the first to go live; Google has not announced a timeline for flight checkout. 

For eligible reservations, users compare options and pay inside Google. The hotel or booking platform remains the merchant of record and handles customer service. But the point of initial contact—and the visual list the user sees—is no longer your site. It is inside an AI-generated response.

This means visibility now depends on two gates instead of one. You must:

- Be integrated with Google as a partner
- Appear in the AI-generated hotel comparison for the user's query

The first gate is clear. The second one is not. [Search Engine Journal's reporting](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-starts-rolling-out-hotel-booking-in-ai-mode/587288/) does not explain how Google orders the hotel options presented to the user, what data partners receive about impressions or clicks, or what criteria determine whether a property is even eligible for the feature. A hypothesis: Google may use the same ranking signals it applies to hotel listings elsewhere in Search (reviews, availability, price competitiveness), but we cannot yet verify that.

## Flight tracking and points are live; flight checkout is not

Google has also added flight price tracking and points-or-miles display to AI Mode. Users can set up price alerts during a conversation and receive email notifications when fares change. The feature uses current prices from more than 300 partner airlines and travel sites. Flight tracking is available in more than 180 countries and territories, though it is excluded from EEA countries and territories.

The points-or-miles feature shows the frequent flyer miles required for supported flights and hotels. Users follow a link to the partner site to complete the redemption. Initial support includes Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines, American Airlines, Choice Hotels, Hilton, and Wyndham. Accor, Flying Blue, Hyatt, LATAM Airlines, and Lufthansa Group are coming in the coming weeks.

Flight checkout—the ability to purchase a ticket inside AI Mode—is stated as a goal by Google but has not yet launched. It remains a hypothesis about the future.

If UCP is not required, the barrier to entry may be lower than expected. If it is, you may need to audit your technical readiness.

## What visibility means when the transaction happens inside the search engine

Hotel booking in AI Mode inverts one assumption about search visibility: traffic is no longer the primary win. A user can complete a booking and never click to your site.

This raises a measurement problem. A hypothesis: Google will eventually offer filtering in Search Console to isolate AI Mode bookings separately from traditional clicks, similar to how you now isolate AI Overview impressions. But that has not yet happened, and we cannot yet verify it will.

What you can measure today is whether your properties appear in the visual list at all. That requires a different frame of mind. You are no longer optimizing for clicks. You are optimizing for the AI's decision to include you in the comparison set.

For booking platforms, the incentive to appear shifts toward:

- Breadth of inventory and availability
- Data completeness (prices, cancellation policies, reviews)
- Pricing competitiveness relative to other options shown
- Quality of user reviews and ratings

For hotel chains, the incentive shifts toward visibility through property management systems that feed Google's integrations, and toward review volume and ratings.

This is closer to how [your competitors appear in AI recommendations](/blog/why-ai-recommends-your-competitors) than how they appear in traditional paid search. You cannot bid your way in. You must be data-ready and integrated.

## Other AI Mode updates: developing-topic carousels and their visibility window

In a separate update, [Google brought developing-topic link carousels to AI Mode](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-brings-developing-topic-link-carousels-to-ai-mode/587022/). These are horizontal rows of article links that appear for trending or breaking-news searches. Google first introduced the format in May for AI Overviews. It is now live in AI Mode as well.

The carousel shows three to five article cards with images, headlines, sources, and publication dates. Google lists Preferred Sources in the carousel when available. The carousel creates larger visual link placement than the smaller citations attached to generated text.

For news and editorial outlets, the relevance is straightforward: Preferred Source status now increases the likelihood of carousel placement in both AI Overviews and AI Mode. The trade-off is that there is no guarantee the carousel will appear for any given developing-topic search. Google says it appears "for only some searches."

Search Console does not yet offer a filter to isolate carousel impressions from other AI Mode impressions, so measuring the impact requires manual tracking or export analysis.

## The unanswered questions that matter most

Google has left several critical details unspecified:

- How are hotel options ranked or ordered in the AI-generated list?
- What analytics do integrated partners receive about impressions, clicks, and bookings completed in AI Mode?
- Are all integrated partners required to support the Universal Commerce Protocol?
- Will Google offer Search Console visibility into AI Mode hotel bookings separately from other traffic?
- When will flight checkout launch, and will the same ranking logic apply?

A hypothesis: Google will announce partner analytics and Search Console features closer to broader rollout, similar to how AI Overview reporting evolved. But we cannot yet verify that.

## What to do now

Hotel booking in AI Mode is live for a limited set of partners and geographies. If you are in that set, the work is not tactical. It is foundational: ensure your data is integrated, complete, and fresh. [Schema markup for AI answers](/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-answers) remains relevant if it helps your hotel data feed into the comparison logic. Most importantly, visibility in AI Mode is no longer a proxy for clicks. It is a direct measure of whether the AI chooses to include you when a user asks about hotels.
