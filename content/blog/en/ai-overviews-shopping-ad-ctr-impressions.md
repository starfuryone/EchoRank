According to [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/ai-overviews-may-be-affecting-shopping-ad-ctr-and-impressions-487265), data from hundreds of advertiser accounts shows Shopping ad click-through rates climbing while impressions decline—a divergence that contradicts the assumption that rising CTR reflects stronger ad performance. Mike Ryan, head of ecommerce at Smarter Ecommerce, theorizes that Google may be selectively showing AI Overviews on queries with lower predicted click intent, reserving Shopping ad placements for high-intent searches. If true, this "experience switching" means fewer ad opportunities overall, but on queries more likely to convert. For advertisers, the implication is urgent: CTR alone no longer tells the story. You need to monitor impressions, clicks, and traffic in concert to understand whether your campaigns are actually gaining ground or simply appearing on fewer, higher-intent queries.

## The pattern: CTR rising, impressions falling

Shopping ad click-through rates climbed roughly 17 to 20 percent year over year, according to benchmark data from Smarter Ecommerce and corroborating analysis from Optmyzr. However, the detail that matters is buried in the aggregate: across thousands of campaigns spanning hundreds of accounts, clicks remained relatively flat or declined slightly, while impressions fell more noticeably. The math is straightforward. When you divide a stable or slightly lower click count by a much smaller impression pool, the ratio improves. CTR rises not because ads perform better, but because they appear less often.

This inversion mirrors what Ryan calls the "crocodile effect" in organic search, where AI Overviews give publishers more impressions but fewer clicks. Shopping ads appear to experience the opposite—fewer impressions, higher CTR—creating what he describes as a "reverse crocodile effect."

## The hypothesis: Google may be choosing between AI Overviews and Shopping ads

Ryan's theory rests on selective query routing. He hypothesizes that Google may be more likely to serve AI Overviews for queries with a lower predicted likelihood of generating an ad click, while preserving Shopping ad placements for searches with stronger click propensity. If Google is already capable of predicting which queries will drive ad engagement, the company could reduce overall Shopping ad impressions without proportionally reducing clicks. Two seemingly contradictory signals would then coexist: AI Overviews suppress some advertising opportunities while remaining ads continue to monetize effectively.

Ryan notes anecdotally that he rarely sees Shopping ads and AI Overviews together on the same search results page—it is typically one or the other. This observation, combined with the data pattern, suggests Google may be becoming more selective about when each experience appears.

## What we cannot yet verify

Ryan is explicit that the data does not prove AI Overviews are causing the change. The timing could be coincidental. AI Overviews could be contributing through a different mechanism. Something else entirely could be responsible for the decline in impressions. The hypothesis remains unconfirmed, and [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/ai-overviews-may-be-affecting-shopping-ad-ctr-and-impressions-487265) treats it as such. No independent third party has validated the theory against Google's actual ranking or serving logic. No LLM researcher outside Google can predict or confirm how the company weights AI Overviews against ad placements on any given query class.

## Why this matters for your visibility strategy

Our recommendation is to stop relying on CTR as your primary signal of campaign health. If higher CTR is driven partly by Google showing Shopping ads less frequently and on more commercially valuable queries, traditional CTR benchmarks no longer reflect campaign reality. A rising CTR could mask a net loss in traffic.

Instead, track three metrics in concert:

- Impression volume (are you appearing less often?)
- Total clicks (is the absolute click count stable, rising, or falling?)
- Traffic and conversion data (does the SERP change translate to business outcomes?)

The broader implication is that AI Overviews are reshaping how Google allocates real estate on the search results page. If Google is indeed choosing between showing an AI Overview or a Shopping ad based on query intent, your campaign's visibility is now partly a function of query classification, not just keyword relevance or bid strength.

This connects to a wider shift in how you should think about visibility itself. When the Reader was human, CTR and impressions were reliable proxies for reach. When the reader includes AI answer engines, traditional search metrics tell an incomplete story. ## The future: "both/and" over "either/or"

Ryan expects the current pattern to be temporary. As Google develops AI-native advertising formats and integrates Shopping ads directly into AI Overviews, the company will likely move from "either/or" search experiences toward "both/and" experiences. That transition means two things: more total real estate for ads and commerce in AI-powered results, but also new competition for attention within an AI Overview itself.

For now, the takeaway is tactical. If your Shopping campaigns are showing higher CTR but lower impressions, the shift may reflect a real change in how Google surfaces your ads to searchers. The rise in CTR is not an unambiguous win. Investigate whether your total clicks, traffic, and revenue have actually improved. If impressions have declined sharply while CTR rose, you are seeing selective routing, not enhanced ad performance.
