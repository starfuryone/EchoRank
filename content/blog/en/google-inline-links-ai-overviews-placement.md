## Google is repositioning inline links in AI-generated responses

[According to Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-inline-links-higher-ai-search-responses/593089/), Google announced five changes to how links appear in AI Mode and AI Overviews. The most immediate change is that inline links—those placed next to relevant text within the AI response—now appear higher in the response body than before. Google reports it has "made continued and substantial increases to the presence of inline links," but did not disclose the size of that increase or the percentage of responses affected.

The company also began testing a new section at the top of certain responses that displays links to official or authoritative sites for task-based searches. This feature targets scenarios where only one or two official sources of information exist. In those cases, Google surfaces those links at the very top of the response and provides context explaining why each link is a resource worth clicking.

Two additional updates affect how links are displayed at the end of AI responses: image thumbnails are now added to related-article suggestions, and carousels highlighting timely articles—filtered by a user's Preferred Sources—are expanding to more regions and languages.

## What inline link placement means for your AI citation rate

Our recommendation: the shift to higher inline link placement may improve click-through behavior, but it does not change your position within AI search results. [According to the announcement](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-inline-links-higher-ai-search-responses/593089/), Google Search Console assigns all inline links within each AI Overview the overview's single position in the search results. Moving a link higher within the response text does not alter that inherited position.

However, the visibility gains are real in a different way. Users scanning an AI response are more likely to see and click links placed near the beginning of the text than links buried deeper. This means higher placement could improve your citation volume without improving your ranking position—a gain worth tracking separately.

Our recommendation: map which of your pages are currently cited in AI responses for your target keywords. You can begin this work by:

- Conducting searches related to your business or topic and noting whether your content appears as an inline link in AI Overviews or AI Mode responses.
- Checking AI-specific visibility metrics using a tool like [/free-audit](/free-audit) to identify gaps between your ranking position and your citation presence.
- Auditing the readability of those cited pages to AI crawlers. If your content isn't being cited now, crawlability issues may be the reason.

## The new official-source section targets high-authority searches

Google is testing a display format for responses to task-based searches where the information landscape is limited. If only one or two official sources can authoritatively answer a query—such as a government agency's tax guidance or an official product manual—Google will test placing those links at the very top of the AI response with explanatory context.

A hypothesis: this test is designed to increase user trust by clearly surfacing authoritative sources before the AI-generated summary. This approach may also reduce liability concerns by ensuring the source of record appears prominently.

We cannot yet verify how often this format will activate or how broadly it will roll out beyond the testing phase. [The announcement does not provide a timeline for broader availability](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-inline-links-higher-ai-search-responses/593089/).

## Preferred Sources expansion affects how users discover your site

The Preferred Sources feature, which allows users to see more content from sites they've explicitly chosen, is expanding to more languages and regions in the carousel format. [Google reported that the number of people using Preferred Sources has increased more than sevenfold since the beginning of the year](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-inline-links-higher-ai-search-responses/593089/), though the company did not disclose the actual user count or the methodology behind that measurement.

Our recommendation: if you manage a website with an audience, encourage your readers to add your site to their Preferred Sources. According to Google's documentation, your site must be included in Search generative AI features via Search Console to qualify. Google provides a button and deep link you can add to your pages to make opting in frictionless.

As Preferred Sources carousels become available in more regions, being chosen as a preferred source could amplify your AI citation frequency in those markets, since your content will be highlighted whenever a user in that group searches for related topics.

## How to prepare for these link visibility changes

The practical impact of these updates depends on where your content currently fits in Google's AI responses:

- **If you are not cited at all**: Focus on crawlability. Ensure your site is readable to AI crawlers by checking your robots.txt, server logs, and page structure. We recommend [reading more about making your site readable to AI crawlers](/blog/make-your-site-readable-to-ai-crawlers) before optimizing content.

- **If you are cited but buried**: Higher inline link placement means deeper citations may now appear lower in responses. Monitor whether your citation rate changes after this update rolls out fully.

- **If you are cited near the top**: The new placement rules may not move your links further up, but the increase in overall inline-link frequency could lift more of your pages into AI responses for related queries.

- **If you serve a niche with limited authoritative sources**: You may qualify for the new official-source section. This format surfaces your link first, before the AI summary, with explanatory text. Ensure your pages are optimized for AI crawling and that they clearly signal their authority level.

The most overlooked action is understanding the gap between your keyword rankings and your AI citations. A page might rank well in traditional blue-link results but never appear in AI Overviews for the same query. [Our guidance on finding your AI citation gap](/blog/find-your-ai-citation-gap) walks through how to identify and close these blind spots.

## What remains unclear about the implementation

[The announcement does not specify the percentage of responses affected by higher inline-link placement](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-inline-links-higher-ai-search-responses/593089/), the timeline for the official-source section rollout, or the specific languages and regions receiving Preferred Sources carousel expansion. Google also did not disclose how it measures the "substantial increases" to inline link presence or provide examples of the performance impact.

A hypothesis: Google may be withholding this data to avoid giving rank trackers and competitors actionable insights into how frequently different sources are being cited. This is consistent with [recent analysis showing Google may be serving misleading search results to rank trackers and AI scraping tools](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/analysis-google-is-poisoning-search-results-for-rank-trackers/592873/).

The timing of these updates—alongside the carousel expansion and the profile-link testing in AI Mode—suggests Google is prioritizing two goals: improving user trust by surfacing authoritative sources, and increasing engagement within AI responses by adding more clickable elements. Whether these changes benefit smaller publishers remains to be seen.
