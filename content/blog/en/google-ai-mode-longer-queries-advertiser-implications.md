## Google's AI Mode is capturing longer, earlier-stage searches—but monetizing them less

[According to Search Engine Journal's report on Google's Ads Decoded episode](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-behavior-shifts-2027-ads-strategy/594971/), people ask 3x longer questions in Google's AI Mode compared to standard Search. Searchers also move between follow-ups, use voice and images, and begin exploring decisions earlier in their purchase journey. Google's Chief Business Officer stated that lower monetization within AI Mode was one of three reasons Search revenue growth slowed to 12% year-over-year in Q3 2026, down from 17% in Q2 and 19% in Q1. [Alphabet's Q3 results show Search revenue was essentially flat quarter-over-quarter at $63.35 billion](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-growth-slows-again-ai-mode/594862/), despite Google reporting the highest query growth rate in five years. The gap between query volume and revenue growth reveals that AI Mode is attracting searches Google hasn't yet figured out how to monetize profitably.

## Longer queries expose customer concerns your product pages don't answer

Google's research shows that people include more context and nuance in AI Mode queries because they can work through complex decisions conversationally. This means the initial search phrase often understates what someone actually wants to know. When a customer asks a three-time-longer question, they're signaling gaps in their understanding or concerns that a standard product description won't resolve.

Our recommendation is to map the follow-up questions customers ask during their research phase, then audit whether your existing content answers them. If a customer's second or third AI Mode question reveals that they're worried about durability, integration with their existing tools, or support quality, a product page that lists features alone will miss the opportunity to address those concerns. Forums, review sites, customer testimonies, and video demonstrations are the places [Shivani Mohan, Google's Search Vice President of Data Science, identified](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-behavior-shifts-2027-ads-strategy/594971/) where people seek "the rich detail, lived experience, and earned authority of the open web."

Consider documenting:

- Common concerns customers raise before buying
- Follow-up questions in your support inbox or sales calls
- Objections your team hears most frequently
- Comparisons customers ask for (your product versus alternatives)
- Limitations or use cases where your offering works best

## AI Mode ads are running across your eligible campaigns—but you can't measure them separately

[Google has made all existing Search, Shopping, and Performance Max campaigns eligible for AI Mode ads by default, with no option to opt out](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-growth-slows-again-ai-mode/594862/). The company also announced ads in AI Mode follow-on questions, meaning customers will see sponsored results as they refine their initial query. Yet when you look at your campaign reporting, AI Mode results are combined with all other Search traffic. You cannot isolate conversion rates, cost-per-acquisition, or lead quality for AI Mode specifically.

[Google's internal data shows click-through rates for sponsored and organic product listings are similar within AI Mode](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-growth-slows-again-ai-mode/594862/), but the company hasn't published conversion benchmarks or how AI Mode visitors compare to traditional Search traffic in terms of actual purchases. This creates a blind spot: you're funding placements in an experience you can measure only in aggregate. If AI Mode attracts exploratory queries that rarely convert, your campaign's overall ROI could deteriorate while appearing stable in standard reporting.

Our recommendation is to track AI Mode performance separately by tagging landing pages or using UTM parameters that let you segment AI Mode clicks in your analytics. Test whether customers who arrive via longer, exploratory AI Mode queries convert at different rates than those from shorter, direct-intent searches. You may also test whether addressing customer concerns directly on landing pages (rather than making visitors dig into FAQ sections) moves exploratory visitors closer to purchase.

## Personal Intelligence now feeds Gmail, Photos, and Drive data into your ads—without new targeting controls

[Google updated its privacy policy in October to allow Gmail, Google Photos, and Google Drive content to influence ads when users connect those apps to Search or Gemini](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-privacy-policy-connected-apps-ai-mode-ads/594546/). Personal Intelligence is an opt-in feature, initially available only to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the U.S., that lets AI Mode reference a flight confirmation in email or travel photos when recommending hotels or luggage. Google is now testing ads within the Personal Intelligence experience.

However, Google has not created any new targeting options, audience segments, or campaign settings for advertisers to reach Personal Intelligence users directly. [The company states that ads aren't matched to raw email or photo content, but instead to summaries, excerpts, and inferences drawn from those sources](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-privacy-policy-connected-apps-ai-mode-ads/594546/). A hypothesis is that this data could influence ad eligibility or ranking within AI Mode, but there's no way for you to confirm it. Google's help documentation also notes that connected-app data may be used to train generative AI models, and that a subset of data is reviewed by human reviewers.

Advertisers cannot see whether Personal Intelligence signals are affecting their campaign performance. The lack of transparency raises a question we cannot yet verify: whether connected-app inferences personalize ads only inside AI Mode or across other Google properties like Discover, Shopping, and Maps.

## Campaign eligibility still depends on broad match and AI-powered strategies

Campaigns using exact match or phrase match keywords cannot serve in AI Mode for exploratory queries. Only campaigns using broad match, AI Max for Search, Shopping, or Performance Max are eligible for both exploratory and direct-intent questions. This means that if you've built your account around precise keyword targeting, you're not reaching the longer, earlier-stage searches Google is seeing in AI Mode.

The structure of your keyword strategy affects whether you can compete for the behavior Google is describing. A hypothesis is that Google designed these eligibility rules to encourage advertisers to adopt its AI-powered bidding strategies, which rely on broad match and rely on Google's systems to match ads to query intent. But we cannot yet verify how eligibility thresholds affect which campaigns actually reach Personal Intelligence users or whether the click-through rates Google cited include traffic from broad match campaigns.

## What remains unanswered for 2027 planning

[The Ads Decoded episode and accompanying posts leave several questions unresolved](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-behavior-shifts-2027-ads-strategy/594971/). Google hasn't shared how longer query behavior varies by industry, product category, or buying stage. The company also hasn't published conversion benchmarks for AI Mode or indicated when separate reporting for AI Mode performance might arrive. Without that data, advertisers are essentially testing their way forward.

For your 2027 strategy, this means:

- Audit your content against the specific concerns customers raise in longer queries
- Test landing page changes that address exploratory questions directly
- Use UTM parameters to segment and track AI Mode traffic separately in your analytics
- Assess whether your keyword strategy (exact, phrase, or broad match) aligns with the query types AI Mode captures
- Monitor impressions and click-through rates for patterns that might signal changes in traffic quality or relevance
