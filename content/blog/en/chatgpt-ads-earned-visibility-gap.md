## Paid and earned visibility serve different purposes in ChatGPT conversations

[According to Search Engine Journal's coverage of a Go Fish Digital and OpenAI webinar](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/chatgpt-ads-and-geo-where-paid-and-earned-ai-visibility-fit-together/590537/), a buyer can research a need, compare options, and complete a purchase without leaving one conversation. An ad can appear alongside an organic answer in that same chat—they reach the same person at the same moment, but they function entirely differently. A paid placement tests an offer during a relevant conversation. An earned mention is a recommendation from the model itself, built on evidence from your site and external sources. Mixing these up is the fastest way to waste budget on paid tactics that can't deliver organic inclusion, or to chase citation campaigns that won't show immediate return.

## Earned mentions require consistent evidence, not single citations

Earned visibility in AI answers is not a rank position. AJ of Go Fish Digital described a framework built on three parts: consistent customer questions, repeated testing, and tracking patterns rather than treating any one answer as final. Start with 20 to 40 questions your actual buyers ask, run them repeatedly over a week or two, and keep your model and settings consistent. Look for gaps that appear across multiple runs instead of betting on a one-off mention.

Patrick Algrim of Go Fish emphasized that the quality of a recommendation matters more than the count of mentions. The evidence available about your products shapes what the system can explain. If your moving company offers cross-country moves but never says so on your own site and has no corroborating evidence elsewhere, an AI system will not infer that service. Your pages need to answer the questions buyers ask, specify what you actually do, and stay consistent with what external sources say about you.

Our recommendation: [structure your content so AI answers can actually use it](/blog/structure-content-for-ai-answers). Unclear or contradictory descriptions block earned visibility more reliably than any outreach campaign can build it.

## Start with your own website, not outreach

Both Go Fish speakers started with the same advice: fix your website first. Algrim pointed out that you can control your own pages. Make sure everything is factually true, connected to a cohesive story about your brand, and clear about what you offer. AJ added a technical first check: ensure the relevant pages are accessible to search crawlers and not blocked by robots rules or a CDN. Reviews, PR, and external mentions still matter, but they cannot rescue an unclear or contradictory account of the business.

This order matters. Outreach campaigns to review sites, Reddit communities, or news outlets will fail if your own site does not answer the question clearly. The sources cited in AI answers are chosen because they contain useful information; if your site is easier to understand and more complete, you have a reason for external sources to link to or reference you.

- Make every product or service page answer the actual customer questions you tracked
- Verify each page is accessible to crawlers (not blocked by robots.txt or CDN restrictions)
- Keep terminology and claims consistent across your site
- Review competitor pages cited in AI answers for your customer questions
- Only then plan outreach to external sources where competitors appear and you do not

## Ad presence in retail ChatGPT conversations reached 22–47% by June

[Comscore's Q2 2026 AI Intelligence Report, reported by Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/chatgpt-ads-appeared-in-47-of-video-gaming-chats-data-shows/590481/), tracked sponsored ads in ChatGPT conversations about retail categories. In gaming conversations, ads appeared in 47% of chats by June, up from 3% in March. Toys and hobbies grew from 5% in March to 24% in June. Books, music, and video rose from 9% in March to 22% in June.

The data covers U.S. PC activity only and is based on an opt-in panel. Comscore's methodology does not specify the exact number of conversations behind each monthly figure or clarify whether one sponsored ad anywhere in a conversation counts as ad presence. The report also does not confirm whether ad-free plan conversations are included in the denominator; if they are, the ad rate among eligible conversations could be higher than reported.

A hypothesis worth testing: [OpenAI's rollout of a beta self-serve Ads Manager on May 5 and the introduction of cost-per-click bidding](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/chatgpt-ads-appeared-in-47-of-video-gaming-chats-data-shows/590481/) may have driven the jump in ad presence in June, the first full month after that rollout. Comscore does not link the two events, and the chart cannot show whether new advertisers, expanded inventory, or other factors caused the increase. Until Comscore releases detailed conversation counts, the June spike should be treated as a preliminary observation, not a trend already locked in.

## Attribution in AI conversations is harder to see in standard reports

GA4's AI-assistant referrals show only part of the customer journey. [According to the webinar coverage](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/chatgpt-ads-and-geo-where-paid-and-earned-ai-visibility-fit-together/590537/), a person may discover a brand in a chatbot, search for it separately, or paste a URL into a browser. That last click to your site appears to come from direct traffic or branded search, not from ChatGPT.

Our recommendation: supplement AI-assistant referrals with customer self-reporting and trends in branded and direct traffic. Keep assistants separate in reports so you can see when direct and branded traffic increase alongside AI mentions. Algrim cautioned against assigning every traffic increase to a single source without evidence. Test specific website or campaign changes over time to build a clearer picture.

[Track your brand mentions across LLMs](/blog/track-brand-mentions-across-llms) on a consistent schedule using the same questions. Record not just whether you appeared, but where and in what context. Compare those results to traffic trends and customer feedback to fill in the gaps that last-click attribution cannot show.

## Where to audit your own citation patterns

[According to Gintare Rimolaityte of Trendos, mapping the sources that inform AI answers in your industry is the first step to understanding where your brand sits](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-to-find-the-sources-shaping-ai-answers-in-your-industry/589756/). Monitoring whether your brand appears in an AI answer is useful, but it does not explain which sources the system actually drew from.

Start with 10 to 20 questions your customers might ask while choosing a product or provider. Run those questions in the engines your buyers use, record the cited sources, and group them by type. Then inspect the pages where competitors appear and your brand does not. If a competitor is cited on a source URL you have not reached, that is a specific opportunity: you have a source, a customer question, and an existing reference to investigate.

Do not give every discovered domain equal priority. Examine citation share and which source categories drive answers for your industry. Chasing thousands of editorial sites indiscriminately wastes time; a focused list of 5–10 sources where competitors are cited gives you a workable starting point.

This audit is a hypothesis: community, user-generated content, and brand-owned pages appear in different proportions across industries and engines. Rimolaityte examined retail, IT services, and communications, and found different mixes in each. Her examples also showed variation across Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, ChatGPT, and Gemini. The practical implication is to keep engine-level results visible in your reporting so you do not lose the detail of which sources matter where.

## Owned content still anchors AI visibility

The most important insight from both webinars is that owned content—your website—remains the foundation. AI systems can recommend you only if you provide clear, accessible evidence of what you do. Paid ads can test an offer, but they cannot substitute for that foundation. External citations matter, but they amplify rather than replace owned content.
