## AI Overviews reduced Wikipedia referrals by roughly 5% after the U.S. rollout

[A University of Washington working paper](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/what-wikipedia-reveals-about-ai-overviews-and-web-traffic/589042/) estimates that Google's AI Overviews reduced monthly search referrals to English Wikipedia by approximately 5% after becoming the U.S. default in May 2024. The study by Mehrzad Khosravi and Hema Yoganarasimhan used Wikipedia's public clickstream data and a difference-in-differences model comparing traffic trends across English, German, and French language editions. The paper has not been peer reviewed. Google disputes whether the combined referral metric isolates the effect of AI Overviews alone.

The revised estimate replaced a larger one from February, reflecting the authors' methodological refinements. Wikimedia confirmed that people access its content through AI systems without visiting wikipedia.org directly. The annual impact for English Wikipedia works out to roughly 1.20 billion fewer search referrals, though this figure assumes the 5% effect remains constant throughout the year.

## The methodology matters because the effect is smaller than headlines suggest

The authors compared English Wikipedia referrals—which came from a region where AI Overviews were on by default—against German and French editions, which served regions without the default feature during the sample period. This approach controls for seasonal and other changes that might have affected all language editions. The model estimated the percentage change in counts using Poisson pseudo-maximum likelihood, which is suited to this type of traffic data. However, a hypothesis is that confounding events in 2024 could still have affected English search differently from German or French search, beyond the AI Overviews rollout.

The paper itself acknowledges a limitation: Wikimedia's public data lumps all external search engines together. Google told the reporting outlet this prevents the analysis from isolating Google specifically. We cannot yet verify whether the 5% decline attributes solely to Google's AI Overviews or includes effects from other search engines. The authors argue that other engines handle only a small portion of traffic and that the timing break created by the May 2024 rollout isolates the effect. A hypothesis is that this reasoning may or may not hold depending on how much non-Google search traffic Wikipedia receives.

## Content type matters more than total traffic decline

[Le Monde's CEO reported that news content traffic remained relatively stable](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/le-monde-says-ai-overviews-havent-hurt-its-audience-so-far/589018/), whereas health, food, and automotive content experienced declines of up to 50%. The difference reflects how AI systems use different content types: news articles serve as previews that drive readers to the full piece, while AI responses to health or product questions may already contain the answer the searcher needs. Le Monde's apps accounted for 36% of traffic in August, and the company views AI Overviews as affecting web traffic more than app-based traffic.

Our recommendation: audit your content by topic and search behavior. News and exploratory content typically withstands AI Overviews better than high-intent queries for specific answers. ## Long-term effects remain unknown

The working paper does not examine whether fewer visits to Wikipedia might affect donation behavior, editing activity, or future content creation. We cannot yet verify whether the 5% decline represents a stable new baseline or is part of a larger trend. Wikimedia's separate data showed overall pageviews declined by about 8% in October 2025 compared to 2024, but this metric reflects bot traffic reclassification and broader social-media shifts, not specifically AI Overviews. The two figures measure different things and cannot be added together.

A hypothesis is that small steady declines in referral traffic, if sustained, compound over years and affect smaller publishers more severely than large ones. Wikipedia has substantial brand recognition and direct traffic. Smaller sites dependent on search referrals face greater risk if the trend widens to their content categories.

## Revenue impact depends on your business model

The University of Washington paper calculated that a hypothetical ad-supported site comparable to Wikipedia might lose between $10.82 million and $37.08 million annually at typical ad rates. This figure is purely illustrative; it does not represent Wikimedia's real finances or any actual revenue shift to Google. Your site's exposure depends on three variables: the share of your traffic that comes from search, how much of your content appears in AI summaries, and whether you monetize referrals through ads or require subscription conversions.

Le Monde's experience underscores this: the company relies on subscriptions and apps, which insulate it from search-referral losses. Visitors from ChatGPT convert to subscribers at rates 26 times higher than from Facebook and 260 times higher than from Discover, showing that some AI sources drive high-value traffic. Le Monde also negotiated content agreements with OpenAI, Perplexity, and Meta that ensure systematic citation and direct links.

Our recommendation is to track which AI systems cite your content and negotiate agreements that include attribution and clickthrough links. Start conversations with AI companies now rather than waiting for referral declines to force the issue.

## Practical steps for your site

- Identify which of your content categories appear in AI Overviews and measure referral changes month to month
- Compare your traffic by content type to identify which topics are most vulnerable
- Audit whether your content is structured so AI systems can parse and cite it properly; see our guide on [how to structure content for AI answers](/blog/structure-content-for-ai-answers)
- Pursue direct agreements with AI systems that cite your work, including links back to your site
- Test whether moving traffic from referrals to subscriptions or direct relationships is viable for your business model
- Monitor [your AI citation gap](/blog/find-your-ai-citation-gap) to understand if you are mentioned without being quoted

## What Google and publishers are saying

Google disputes the methodology used in the University of Washington study, arguing that the combined search-engine referral metric cannot isolate AI Overviews' effect. Wikimedia acknowledged that people access content through AI without visiting wikipedia.org, framing the question differently: the paper measures one specific referral pathway, not total reach.

The French press alliance APIG cited Arcom's estimate of 33% to 38% traffic loss in European markets where AI Overviews were live and filed a complaint with France's competition authority. Le Monde's CEO acknowledged sharing APIG's views on algorithm transparency and neighboring rights but noted that his company's own data showed no sudden audience collapse. He emphasized that search referrals represent roughly half of traffic to major news sites, so even a 30% decline in search traffic translates to a smaller overall decrease.

Neither Google nor the studies provide details on long-term trends, whether the effect stabilizes, or whether publishers can adapt their content strategy to recover referrals. A hypothesis is that sites that successfully position content for AI citation will see less referral decline than those that do not.

## The broader visibility question

The real shift is not whether referrals rise or fall, but whether your brand and content remain visible in AI-powered search. Being cited in an AI Overview without a clickthrough link means your brand is credited but your business is not funded. Our recommendation is to monitor both metrics: referrals from traditional search and citations in AI systems. These are now separate channels requiring separate strategies.

Use your site audit to establish a baseline, then track both search traffic and AI visibility over the next quarter. If referrals decline in your category, experiment with content formats that AI systems cite more frequently, such as structured definitions, lists, and comparisons.
