## Google is widening ads in AI Mode, and reporting still lags behind

Google is expanding ads in AI Mode to more than 10 new countries, with the initial rollout supporting English. It is also launching multiturn ads in the U.S., which can appear on follow-up questions within an AI Mode conversation. According to [Search Engine Journal's report on the update](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-expands-ads-in-ai-mode-multiturn-ads/596540/), Google shared the news on November 9, and eligible campaigns can already participate. The main limitation for advertisers is that AI Mode performance cannot be viewed separately in Google Ads.

## What is changing, and where

Google's October announcement named the additional countries in its initial expansion. The reporting says Brazil, Mexico and the United Kingdom are planned for Q4 2026 rather than part of the first wave. Pakistan supports text ads only, according to Google's availability documentation as cited by the same source.

Multiturn ads are the more consequential change for planners. Google describes AI Mode as suited to exploratory questions, detailed comparisons and follow-ups. Under the multiturn format, an ad can appear during those follow-up exchanges when the conversation has commercial relevance. The source notes that Google hasn't said when this will extend beyond the U.S.

### How the ad can be matched

The reporting describes two matching routes. Ads can match directly to a search query, or they can draw on context from both the user's question and the AI response. That second route means an exploratory question can create an ad opportunity even when the user has not searched for a specific product. [Search Engine Journal's analysis of Google's upper-funnel comments](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/paid-search-is-moving-up-the-funnel-google-admits-ai-mode-pays-less/595875/) makes the same point from another angle: many AI Mode interactions are early in the buying process, so the ads may reach people who are still comparing options.

## What reporting actually shows

Here the picture is narrower. Google's reporting documentation, as summarized by the source, says AI Mode impressions, clicks, conversions and costs are included in standard campaign reporting. Search terms are combined with other traffic. There is no separate AI Mode breakdown, which means an AI Mode-specific CPA or ROAS cannot be calculated.

The same source notes that reported search terms for experiences including AI Mode may approximate the user's intent rather than show the exact question entered. Advertisers should not assume they are seeing verbatim queries.

Our recommendation is to tell clients exactly this: AI Mode results sit inside campaign totals, and Google Ads will not show how much spend or how many conversions came from that surface. Teams can still review search terms for relevance, compare conversion performance and check lead quality. If results shift after the rollout, the timing alone cannot establish that AI Mode caused the change.

## The up-funnel question

The second source in this news cluster reports a tension worth keeping in view. Google's head of search data science described people arriving at Google earlier in their buying journeys with longer, more detailed questions. On the same day, [Alphabet's chief business officer said AI Mode queries drive lower monetization than traditional searches](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/paid-search-is-moving-up-the-funnel-google-admits-ai-mode-pays-less/595875/), according to that report. The author's view is that the planning problem is that queries are arriving at the top of the funnel while most accounts are still judged at the bottom.

We would not read the monetization comment as a forecast for your account. The source reports Google's own statement and Google's own internal click-through comparison, covering a short window, and it has not been verified independently.

### A checklist for the next client conversation

- Confirm which campaigns are eligible and whether they are running in an English-language market covered by the rollout.
- Explain that AI Mode performance is reported inside campaign totals, not as its own line.
- Review search terms for relevance, knowing they may approximate user intent.
- Judge AI Mode traffic on lead quality as well as volume, since a longer question does not signal purchase readiness.
- Avoid attributing overall campaign changes to AI Mode without a controlled comparison.

## Google's asset tooling is moving the same direction

A related change appeared in the same week. [Search Engine Journal reported that some accounts are showing a beta Asset activity page](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ads-tests-asset-activity-page-with-weekly-ai-insights/596442/) in the Assets section of Google Ads. It carries weekly AI-generated insight cards about ad creative, including suggested headline themes and notes on top-performing headlines. The report says Google had not published help documentation about the feature as of November 9, and it is unclear which accounts receive it.

The same source notes that Google already lists CTR, CPC, CPA and ROAS at the asset level as "directional indicators only." Our recommendation is to use any CTR-based card as a starting point for a test, then judge the result at the asset group or campaign level. Check how a suggested headline converts in your own account before keeping it.

## What is still unknown

- How AI Mode ads perform against traditional Search ads. Google has shared one click-through comparison covering a two-week internal window. We cannot yet verify it outside Google.
- Whether multiturn ads will expand beyond the U.S., and on what timeline. Google has not said.
- Whether separate AI Mode reporting will arrive. The reporting does not indicate a date.
- Which accounts will see the Asset activity page, and how its cards are generated. As of this writing, no help page explains either.

We do not have verified answers to these. Stated as a hypothesis, the likeliest near-term effect is that teams are asked to explain AI Mode results with the same campaign-level data they already have, which puts more weight on conversion quality checks.
