## Google AI Mode rarely shows carousel products—and charges different prices when it does

[According to Search Engine Journal reporting on Productrise data](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ai-mode-prices-differ-from-product-carousel-for-same-items/588227/), Google's AI Mode displays fundamentally different shopping results than the Popular products carousel in regular search. Only 1.28% of carousel products appear in AI Mode for the same query on the same day. Among products that do overlap, the first-listed seller changes 49.6% of the time, and prices differ in 38.1% of matched products. When AI Mode does show a different price, it is higher 68.4% of the time, with an average difference of 21.6% across all matched products. This gap matters because shoppers see the first-listed offer before clicking deeper to compare alternatives.

## Why the product lists diverge so sharply

Productrise tracked over 2 million product listings across more than 100,000 searches in the US and UK from August 9 to August 31, 2026. The company monitored identical product-specific searches in both AI Mode and regular search, comparing products using Google's product ID and tracking the first-listed seller and price. When both systems returned results, the carousel displayed an average of 27.8 products per query while AI Mode presented only 3.9. The sheer difference in result volume—and the fact that [the two systems rarely show the same products at all](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ai-mode-prices-differ-from-product-carousel-for-same-items/588227/)—suggests they operate on different ranking logic.

Productrise founder Hugo Huijer interpreted the pattern to mean that "the cheapest price is less of a factor in AI Mode." This is a hypothesis worth testing, but Google has not confirmed how AI Mode selects products or merchants. Google responded to the reporting by stating that both systems use the same Shopping Graph data source and that shoppers can click through to compare prices across retailers. That response does not explain why AI Mode consistently favors different merchants or higher prices when the underlying data is identical.

Our recommendation is to stop treating the carousel and AI Mode as interchangeable ranking surfaces. They behave like separate products with separate selection criteria, even though they feed from the same data. If your brand appears in one and not the other, the reason is not data quality or feed completeness—it is algorithmic preference.

## Price differences favor AI Mode listings, not shoppers

Among products that appeared in both locations, prices matched only 61.9% of the time. Where they differed, AI Mode's higher price appeared in 68.4% of instances. The median premium was 22.2%, though the average of 88.5% suggests outliers—such as comparing a used item in the carousel to a new item in AI Mode—inflated the mean. When AI Mode prices were lower, the median difference was 7.8%, narrower than the upside.

Across all matched products, AI Mode averaged 21.6% higher prices than the carousel. This pattern holds even though clicking either product opens a panel showing other sellers and prices. The first-listed offer is the path of least resistance. Shoppers who convert from the initial display price never see the alternative merchants. The data does not capture clicks or purchases, so we cannot verify whether shoppers actually buy at the first-listed price or proceed to compare. But the mere presence of a higher price in the prominent position creates friction between shopper expectations and merchant positioning.

## What merchants should track now

- Audit your products in both AI Mode and the Popular carousel for the same high-volume queries
- Check whether your brand appears first, second, or not at all in each surface
- Record the price displayed in each location and compare it to your feed submission
- Test whether your product data includes rich attributes (size, color, material, condition) that might influence AI Mode selection
- Monitor whether AI Mode favors certain merchant types or fulfillment models over others

Google has not disclosed the selection criteria for AI Mode shopping results, and the company has not verified Productrise's data. Our recommendation is to treat this gap as a live experiment in your own account. Search for your top products in both locations yourself, note which merchants appear first, and compare prices. Log what you find alongside the date and query. If a pattern emerges—your competitor appears in AI Mode while you don't, or your price is consistently higher—flag it for your feed optimization and merchant center strategy.

## What remains unclear

The Productrise data represents product-specific searches, not a random sample of all Google Shopping queries. The company's tracking focuses on products it monitors through its platform, which may skew toward categories where feeds are complex or high-value. We cannot yet verify whether the 1.28% overlap and 21.6% price premium hold across all product categories or only in the ones Productrise analyzed. It is also stated as a hypothesis that AI Mode deprioritizes price in favor of other signals like feed richness or brand reputation; the data describes the outcome but not the mechanism.

Google's support page for AI Mode includes a Merchant Center insights report showing your share of voice as a percentage of AI impressions, but it does not yet break down which seller or price AI Mode displays for your products. That transparency gap means you cannot diagnose why your products rank differently without running your own manual searches. A second uncertainty is whether this pricing behavior is intentional or a side effect of AI Mode's model selection. [Google has added Gemini 3.8 Flash to AI Mode's model menu](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-gemini-3-8-flash-ai-mode/588194/) on the same day it released the model, and the model behind AI Mode changes every few weeks. Testing conducted on 3.7 Flash three weeks ago was testing a different model than one run on 3.8 Flash today. It is possible that price anomalies will shift as the underlying model changes, or that they reflect tuning specific to a particular version.

## How AI Mode fits into your visibility strategy

AI Mode is accessible with a single click from any product search, and it appears as a tab above regular results. Shoppers can run the same search in both locations and compare what each surface recommends. Unlike the carousel, which is driven by engagement and relevance, AI Mode's selection remains opaque. The two surfaces are now distinct ranking arenas, each with its own merchant winners and losers.

Your merchant center data and shopping feed are the same on both sides. The difference is how each system weights that data. For now, Productrise's data gives you a baseline: AI Mode and the carousel are not the same product. Assume they will stay different, monitor your own listings in both places, and adjust your feed strategy accordingly. The price premium in AI Mode may reflect AI's deference to quality or brand signals, or it may reflect an opportunity for lower-priced competitors to win visibility. Either way, you need to know where your products land and at what price before you can optimize.
