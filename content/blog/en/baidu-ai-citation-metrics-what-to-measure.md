Baidu has added AI Traffic Analysis to its webmaster tools, launching a citation dashboard that tracks how often AI answers cite your pages. [According to Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/baidu-now-measures-ai-citations-heres-what-seos-should-and-shouldnt-read-into-it/590276/), Chinese webmasters began noticing the feature around September 15, with official confirmation on September 16. The catch: data isn't live yet, though dashboards are rolling out in phases. What Baidu chose to measure, however, tells us something concrete about how AI visibility differs from traditional search visibility—and why you need to read the fine print before acting on it.

## Baidu measures citations and impressions as separate things

Baidu's AI Traffic Analysis dashboard tracks four metrics: citations, impressions, clicks, and AI CTR. The critical detail is how AI CTR is calculated. Baidu divides clicks by citations, not clicks by impressions. This is fundamentally different from standard search console logic, where CTR is clicks divided by impressions. The difference matters because it suggests a page can be cited without its link being visible—for example, in a collapsed source list. Our recommendation: calculate both figures yourself and watch the gap between citations and impressions. That gap may become one of the most informative signals available.

The platform also includes a citation trend chart and tables of top keywords and top pages, each capped at the top 50,000 entries. Data updates daily and reflects the previous day's activity, though Baidu warns that updates may be delayed.

## The scope is narrower than you might think

[Search Engine Journal notes](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/baidu-now-measures-ai-citations-heres-what-seos-should-and-shouldnt-read-into-it/590276/) that Baidu defines the data as covering only the "traditional search scenario"—AI answers within Baidu's regular search results. Some Chinese commentators have assumed the report includes Baidu's standalone AI products like Baidu Wenxin, but the official wording does not support that.

More importantly, the report cannot cover China's fragmented AI search market. Doubao, DeepSeek, Qwen/Quark, and Kimi operate their own ecosystems without sharing data. For many categories, those platforms may matter more than Baidu's AI answers. This is a hypothesis you should test: assume Baidu's citation patterns apply broadly, then verify them on competing platforms before committing resources.

## Where the real value lives: the keyword table

The keyword table is where actionable demand data emerges. It will show which queries trigger AI answers that cite your content. This information is unavailable anywhere else in China through first-party reporting.

Chinese SEOs are already discussing using these keywords to guide optimization on other AI platforms. Our recommendation is to treat Baidu's data as a hypothesis to test elsewhere, not a rule to copy. Each platform has different citation preferences. A query that generates citations on Baidu may behave very differently on DeepSeek or Kimi.

Consider this approach:

- Identify high-volume keywords that trigger AI citations on Baidu
- Test those keywords on competing platforms to see citation patterns
- Compare citation rates across platforms to find platform-specific preferences
- Adjust content strategy based on what you learn, not on Baidu data alone
- Monitor which platforms matter most for your category and your audience

## What this means for AI visibility measurement

Baidu's move is significant because it puts the company among the few search engines that report AI citations as a separate category. Most platforms fold AI citations into general search performance or don't report them at all. For international brands operating in China, this is rare first-party visibility data in a market where you normally rely on third-party estimates.

The decision to track citations separately from impressions also reveals something about how Baidu thinks about AI search. It treats AI answers and their sources as a distinct performance channel. This aligns with our earlier work on [how to measure AI search visibility without guessing](/blog/how-to-measure-ai-search-visibility), which emphasized that AI metrics and traditional search metrics measure different things.

The move also signals renewed attention to Baidu's webmaster platform. The platform has seen few meaningful updates in recent years, and practitioners have openly questioned whether Baidu still prioritizes it. An AI citation report changes that equation. It gives site owners a reason to maintain their Baidu verification and to think of Baidu as a place where AI visibility can actually be measured and tracked.

## What remains uncertain

We cannot yet verify how Baidu determines which sources appear in AI answers or how the ranking of sources works. The dashboard will show that you were cited, but not why a citation happened or how it compared to competing sources. This is a limitation worth flagging: visibility data without ranking context is useful for demand research but less useful for diagnostics.

It is also unknown whether Baidu will add comparison features or benchmarking data. Will you be able to see how your citation share compares to competitors in your category? Standard search consoles have added these features over time. If Baidu follows suit, the data becomes much more actionable.

A hypothesis also worth testing: as the data becomes live and more detailed, will patterns emerge that let you predict which content structures or formats perform better in AI answers? [Structuring content for AI answers](/blog/structure-content-for-ai-answers) has broad principles, but Baidu's keyword-level data might reveal platform-specific preferences.

## Next steps for SEOs tracking AI visibility

Start by verifying your Baidu account and ensuring your site is properly claimed in the webmaster tools. Once data goes live, check the keyword table to see which of your queries generate AI citations. Use [Echorank's free audit](/free-audit) to cross-check your AI visibility across other platforms and understand where you stand in relation to your competitors.

Compare Baidu's citation data against your own impressions and CTR from the same platform. Look for gaps between citations and impressions, and investigate which content types or formats appear most often in AI answers. Then test those patterns on other Chinese AI platforms to see if they hold.

Remember: Baidu's data is first-party and specific to its search ecosystem. It is a hypothesis about demand and content performance, not a universal rule. Use it to inform your strategy, but validate it on the platforms where your audience actually searches.
