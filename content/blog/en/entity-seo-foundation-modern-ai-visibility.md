## Entity SEO is how AI systems understand who you are

Entity SEO is the practice of reducing ambiguity around the people, places, organizations, products, events and topics that matter to your business. [According to Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/entities-are-the-foundation-of-modern-seo/587886/), Google's Knowledge Graph—a database of entities that has existed for 15 years—is the most obvious system that represents entities and their relationships. Modern LLMs operate the same way. If search engines and AI systems cannot confidently understand who you are from fragmented signals across the web, it becomes harder for them to correctly interpret and connect those signals when ranking, recommending or citing you. Entity SEO is not new, but it is now the foundation of AI visibility.

## Why entity clarity matters to LLMs and search engines

[Search Engine Journal explains](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/entities-are-the-foundation-of-modern-seo/587886/) that search engines and AI systems build a picture of who you are from scattered evidence across the web. Your job is to make that picture as clear, consistent and well-supported as possible. Without clarity, systems cannot confidently understand your brand or connect related signals. On-page content, internal and external links, navigation, and your owned and earned consistency all matter. The more corroborated your identity is—the more third parties say what you say about yourself—the more confident these systems become that you are who you claim to be.

This is why our recommendation is straightforward: start with what you control. Consistency across your owned properties—your website, social profiles, business registrations—is the foundation. If you cannot ensure accuracy on first pass, AI crawlers may not give you a second chance to correct misunderstandings.

## Build entity hygiene across owned properties

Entity hygiene means removing ambiguity and inconsistency from the information systems can extract about you. [Search Engine Journal recommends a framework with four elements](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/entities-are-the-foundation-of-modern-seo/587886/): Own, Describe, Prove, and Measure. The first three are entirely in your control. The fourth tells you whether it's working.

Start here:

- Audit all your owned properties: website, social profiles, business directories, author bios, leadership pages
- Document your core identity signals: Name, Address, Phone Number (NAP), organization type, key personnel, brand description
- Ensure consistency across every property you edit
- Verify that internal links point to the correct endpoints
- Check that descriptions of what you do align across all properties

This is unsexy work. It will not deliver a dramatic traffic spike. But it removes the friction that prevents AI systems from confidently understanding and citing you.

## Owned properties are your only controllable lever

[According to Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/entities-are-the-foundation-of-modern-seo/587886/), everything you classify under Own, Describe, and Prove falls under your control. These are all editable properties. Earned properties—what others say about you—matter enormously, but they are not editable. Third-party mentions, news coverage, and backlinks help corroborate your identity. But you cannot send an email to every publication and guarantee it will be acted upon.

Our recommendation: focus almost all of your effort on your owned properties. This is particularly important for smaller brands and publishers who do not have the trust capital of the BBC or New York Times. If your brand name is spelled inconsistently, if your address changes between properties, if your description shifts, these gaps create ambiguity that AI systems struggle to resolve.

Once owned properties are consistent and accurate, then you can think about how to influence earned properties. But start with what you can fix directly.

## Track which entities are talking about your brand

[Search Engine Journal notes](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/entities-are-the-foundation-of-modern-seo/587886/) that metrics like branded search volume have always been important to track, as is direct traffic to specific sections of your site. You need to understand where you have real user demand. The same principle applies to entity visibility: you should understand which types of places, organizations, or events associate with your brand in the eyes of AI systems.

Our recommendation is to use [our free audit](/free-audit) to identify where your brand appears, how consistently it appears, and which signals might be creating confusion. This reveals your entity drift—the gap between how you describe yourself and how you are described elsewhere.

## Entity ambiguity prevents confident citation

A hypothesis worth testing: AI systems cite sources more confidently when they have a clear, corroborated picture of who that source is. If a system cannot confidently disambiguate your organization from a similarly named one, or if your entity profile is scattered and inconsistent, the system may avoid citing you altogether. We cannot yet verify this inside any LLM's ranking logic, but it aligns with how knowledge graphs and entity resolution systems are documented to work.

This connects to a larger visibility problem: being mentioned everywhere but quoted nowhere. [Our earlier guide on finding your AI citation gap](/blog/find-your-ai-citation-gap) explores this in detail. Entity clarity is one of the foundational practices that moves you from mentions to citations.

## News and short-tail entity searches demand accuracy

[Search Engine Journal points out](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/entities-are-the-foundation-of-modern-seo/587886/) that news searches are dominated by entities. Headlines, opening paragraphs, images and page structure all need a clear focal point—a person, place, event or organization. For news publishers, Google's news crawling system may crawl your article once and then only again when demand spikes. Accuracy on the first pass is critical.

The same pressure applies to any content targeting short-tail entity searches. Users hear about a story and search for the person or event. If your content does not immediately establish what it is about, AI systems struggle to connect it to the right entity in their knowledge base.

## Start with owned properties, then measure

Do not attempt to build a grandiose entity strategy before your owned properties are clean. The foundation is boring but necessary:

1. Make a list of everything you own: website sections, social profiles, directory listings, author pages, product pages
2. Verify that NAP (Name, Address, Phone Number) is consistent across all properties
3. Check that descriptions of who you are align
4. Ensure internal links point to the correct endpoints
5. Document leadership and key contributors consistently
6. Review which third-party properties mention you and flag inconsistencies

Once entity hygiene is established, [structure your content so AI systems can actually use it](/blog/structure-content-for-ai-answers). This means clarity in headings, schema markup where appropriate, and consistency in how you refer to yourself and related entities.

The work is practical and unglamorous. But it directly addresses why AI systems and search engines often recommend your competitors instead: they have a clearer picture of who those competitors are.
