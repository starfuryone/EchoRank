An AI answer mentions your brand. That looks like progress, but it does not tell you what to do next. [According to Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-cites-your-brand-how-to-choose-your-next-move/589990/), Constance Tan, Product Marketer at Ahrefs, outlined a framework to separate the useful signals from the noise. The starting point is not a single reassuring mention—it is recurring patterns across the customer journey. A competitor may appear more often, the answer may describe an old version of your product, or important pages may never reach the bots collecting information. Each problem requires a different response.

## One mention is not a strategy

Looking for your brand in a single AI prompt or session is not a strategy. Generating hundreds of prompts does not guarantee useful coverage either. [Tan organized monitoring around three kinds of questions](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-cites-your-brand-how-to-choose-your-next-move/589990/) that run through the customer journey: search queries, support conversations, and sales questions relevant to your market. Community discussions—Reddit, Quora, forums, and niche platforms—supply real language people use. Tan cautioned that the most useful forums differ by market; Reddit and Quora are not the answer everywhere.

The goal is clear: build a representative view from every angle of how customers encounter your category. Monitor that set over time. Repeated sources, positioning claims, and factual errors give you something concrete to investigate rather than a vague sense of being overlooked.

## Understand the gap before you act

If competitors appear more often in AI answers, share of voice identifies the gap. Reading those answers helps explain it. Does AI repeatedly favor another brand for small businesses or ecommerce? Which pages supply those distinctions? Does your content explain the same use cases?

Our recommendation: examine both the sources and their formats. Reviews, discussions, and videos can matter alongside articles. For outreach, prioritize pages cited repeatedly and authors you can realistically reach. Domain strength and organic traffic add context, but an influential competitor-owned page may offer little opportunity for a correction.

## Fix your own house first

Before contacting anyone, [inspect your own pricing pages, product explanations, profiles, and older posts](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-cites-your-brand-how-to-choose-your-next-move/589990/). Conflicting information can leave AI answers describing features or plans that have changed. This is not a content problem you can solve by outreach—it is a problem you created.

Missing citations can have a different cause entirely: bots may be unable to retrieve your content. Check for firewall restrictions, broken URLs, timeouts, and pages that rely on JavaScript to display important information. Investigate those failures before treating every visibility gap as a content problem. A useful page cannot serve as a retrieved source if the bot cannot access its information.

Our recommendation: run your site through a [free audit](/free-audit) to surface crawlability issues that may be blocking AI systems from seeing your content.

## Outreach has limits; know when to skip it

Third-party corrections require patience and realism. [Tan shared an Ahrefs outreach example: the team contacted 26 authors about inaccurate information, 10 replied, and four updated their content](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-cites-your-brand-how-to-choose-your-next-move/589990/). One issue concerned older descriptions of which plans included API access. Those are outreach outcomes, not proof of a corresponding lift in citations or revenue. They illustrate why selecting reachable, frequently cited sources matters.

Updating someone else's page is often not practical. [Tan explained the alternative: create new sources of information, new pages that answer or cover the topic in a better way, more comprehensively, or with more up-to-date information](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-cites-your-brand-how-to-choose-your-next-move/589990/). A warm relationship can make a correction worthwhile. An important topic with weak coverage may justify an original guide or collaboration. If the same error appears across several sources, one new article will not be enough.

## Participation is not pitching

[Tan's advice for community participation was not to insert a product pitch into every thread](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-cites-your-brand-how-to-choose-your-next-move/589990/). Answer technical questions, correct factual mistakes, or offer useful guidance. Recurring complaints can reveal product or onboarding problems worth taking back to the teams that can fix them. That signal matters more than another mention.

## A practical starting checklist

Before you decide whether to chase citations, run through these steps:

- Establish what questions AI is answering about your category (search, support, sales, community).
- Monitor those questions monthly and look for patterns, not single mentions.
- Identify which sources appear repeatedly in AI answers and whether they mention you or competitors.
- Check your own pages for outdated claims, conflicting information, and broken links.
- Test whether AI crawlers can actually retrieve your important pages (firewall, JavaScript, timeouts).
- Compare the effort of outreach against the reachability and frequency of the source you want to correct.
- Consider whether a new, comprehensive page might work better than asking someone else to edit theirs.
- Look at reviews, discussions, and videos—not just articles—as sources that shape AI answers.

## What is still unclear

A hypothesis worth testing: [Tan discussed Ahrefs' self-reported discovery data, including customers who mentioned ChatGPT, rather than claiming a revenue-per-citation formula](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-cites-your-brand-how-to-choose-your-next-move/589990/). Her recommendation was to consider impressions and share of voice alongside conversions, sales, and customer attribution information. We cannot yet verify a direct link between AI citation counts and revenue. That gap means you should track visibility and share of voice while you measure actual business outcomes separately. Attribution models will improve, but they are not yet reliable enough to guide strategy alone.

The framework Tan outlined moves you away from reactive response to deliberate diagnosis. You are looking for patterns, not reassurance. You are checking your own access before blaming the outside world. You are weighing the cost of outreach against the likelihood it will move the needle. That clarity is the real win.
