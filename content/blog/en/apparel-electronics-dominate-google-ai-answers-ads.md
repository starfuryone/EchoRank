## Apparel and electronics account for 95% of Google AI answer ads because they align with buying intent

[Adthena's Holiday AI Paid Search Report](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ai-answer-ads-apparel-electronics-report/595984/) found approximately 175,100 paid ad placements across Google's AI Overview and AI Mode panels in the U.S. from June to September 2026. Apparel represented 111,000 placements; consumer electronics, 55,000. Travel, beauty, toys, and games combined for fewer than 10,000. The data covers five holiday shopping categories, but the dominance of two categories is striking. This concentration is not accidental. Apparel and electronics are high-intent categories where users make purchasing decisions. Google's AI answers appear most often when search intent is strongest.

## What this concentration means for your AI visibility

Our recommendation is to check whether your products automatically appear in AI answers at all. All Search, Shopping, and Performance Max campaigns are [automatically eligible](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ai-answer-ads-apparel-electronics-report/595984/) to show in Google's AI-powered experiences. You cannot target AI Overviews or AI Mode specifically, and you cannot opt out entirely—only from Search itself. This means if your apparel or electronics inventory is in Google Shopping or Performance Max, it may already be appearing in AI answers without your knowledge.

If your category is not apparel or electronics, this data raises a sharper question: are users seeing AI answers for your product category at all? Categories with fewer than 2,000 ad placements (beauty, toys, games) may be under-served by AI answers themselves. Our recommendation: use [/free-audit](/free-audit) to check how often AI answers appear for your target keywords, and whether your site is cited in them.

## Comparison queries drive ads in beauty and travel, but not electronics

The same report reveals a second pattern: ad placement varies by query type. Beauty ads appear on "research and discovery" queries (where users compare options) 83% of the time. Travel and toys show these query types 69% and 67% of the time. Apparel and electronics drop to 48% and 42% respectively.

This suggests that shoppers searching for electronics and clothing use direct product names and specifications more often than comparison language. Beauty shoppers ask "what's the best moisturizer?" Travel shoppers ask "where should I go in October?" Electronics shoppers ask "gaming laptop specs" or "iPhone 16 price."

Our recommendation: if you sell apparel or electronics, audit your content strategy for comparison and best-of queries. [Structure your content to answer comparison questions](/blog/structure-content-for-ai-answers) so your brand surfaces when users do ask "best winter coat" or "which laptop for video editing." If you sell beauty or travel, you are already winning comparison queries; focus instead on brand visibility in product-specific queries.

## Categories outside apparel and electronics face growing visibility gaps

The ad concentration tells us where Google's AI answers are working hardest. It also tells us where they are absent. A site selling beauty products, toys, or travel experiences competes in a space with minimal AI answer ad activity. This creates two risks.

First, a hypothesis: categories with fewer AI ads may receive fewer AI Overview impressions overall, which would mean fewer users seeing any answers at all. The report does not state which queries triggered AI answers or whether the five categories received equal visibility. Second, if your category is under-represented, you have less direct competition in AI answers but also less traffic. Sites in these categories should monitor how often AI answers appear for their keywords.

Our recommendation: [track whether your brand is mentioned but not quoted in AI answers](/blog/find-your-ai-citation-gap). Mention without citation means AI models know about you but are not directing users to you. This often precedes citation.

## Google AI answer ads scaled dramatically; ChatGPT ads are growing faster

For context on scale: Google reported an 89% increase in U.S. AI Overview appearances from August 2025 to August 2026. Globally, growth was steeper. The UK and EU saw 160% growth; Australia, 536%.

By contrast, [ChatGPT Ads saw rapid advertiser growth](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ai-answer-ads-apparel-electronics-report/595984/). In June 2026, 1,082 advertisers ran ads across five categories. By August, that number reached 6,333. OpenAI later announced (on October 21) that more than 100,000 total advertisers have run ChatGPT campaigns across all categories since the feature launched. The velocity differs: Google's AI answers show slower but steadier growth; ChatGPT ads accelerated sharply once advertisers understood the value.

Our recommendation: do not assume Google AI answers are your only AI visibility channel. ChatGPT ads may not send as much traffic as Google today, but the advertiser base is growing faster. Understand [how to measure AI search visibility](/blog/how-to-measure-ai-search-visibility) across both surfaces so you know where your traffic actually comes from.

## What remains unknown about this data

A hypothesis: the report counts ad placements, not impressions, clicks, or conversions. Adthena identified where ads appeared, but the data does not show whether users clicked them or whether those clicks converted to sales. An ad that appears 1,000 times and receives no clicks has zero business value. Without click data, we cannot measure whether AI answer ads are actually working.

Second, a hypothesis: we do not know whether the same queries were tracked across all four months (June–September 2026). The report groups data by category but does not describe the queries behind those impressions. If tracking methodology shifted, or if OpenAI and Google added new markets mid-period, the month-over-month growth rates may not be comparable.

Third, stated as a hypothesis: the report does not explain how Adthena categorized queries as "research and discovery" versus direct product searches. This categorization drives the finding about comparison queries, but without knowing the rules, we cannot assess whether other categories would show different patterns under different definitions.

## Actions to take today

- Verify whether your Shopping or Performance Max campaigns are running and eligible for AI answer placement.
- Search for your target keywords in Google and ChatGPT and note how often AI answers appear (and whether your site is cited).
- If your category is apparel or electronics, audit your top 50 keywords for comparison language and create content around "best" and "vs." queries.
- If your category is beauty, travel, toys, or games, monitor AI answer frequency for your keywords—lower placement counts may signal lower impression volume.
- Log into your Google Ads account and check your placement reports for impressions in "AI-powered experiences" to see whether you're already running there.
