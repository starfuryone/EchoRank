## Fiverr's survey shows GEO outpacing SEO in client demand

[According to Fiverr's 2026 Holiday Marketing Index released on September 21](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/fiverr-survey-search-freelancers-geo-demand/590403/), sixty-seven percent of search freelancers report an increase in generative engine optimization requests over the past year. By comparison, fifty-six percent saw rising demand for Google ranking work. The findings highlight a market shift toward AI answer engines, yet specialists still view traditional SEO as the stronger long-term play. Fiverr's internal marketplace data shows GEO services grew by twenty-nine percent from November 2025 to April 2026. The gap between rising GEO demand and persistent SEO confidence reflects the current state of search strategy: both matter, but their relative importance is in flux.

## GEO demand climbs, but SEO remains the preferred long-term bet

Sixty-seven percent reported more client requests for GEO work than the prior year, yet only fifty-six percent saw comparable growth in Google SEO tasks. The reversal is striking—AI answer engines are now driving more new work than traditional search ranking. [The report advises funding both](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/fiverr-survey-search-freelancers-geo-demand/590403/), and that guidance aligns with what we're observing across the market. Our recommendation: do not treat GEO and SEO as mutually exclusive. Your content strategy should address both simultaneously, because clients are requesting GEO work at higher rates precisely because they recognize the risk of invisibility in AI answers.

When asked about long-term value, the picture shifts. Sixty-one percent of search specialists believe Google SEO remains the strongest investment. This suggests that while GEO demand is accelerating, the market has not yet reached consensus that AI answer engines will displace Google as the primary discovery mechanism. A hypothesis worth testing: freelancers may be responding to immediate client requests for GEO work while privately doubting its durability. Or they may simply view SEO as lower-risk because its returns are more predictable and measurable.

## AI involvement triggers steep pricing pressure on search work

Sixty-one percent of search specialists report that clients expect lower prices simply because AI is involved in the deliverable. [Search specialists experience the greatest pricing pressure among all surveyed groups](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/fiverr-survey-search-freelancers-geo-demand/590403/), more than e-commerce or user-generated content freelancers. The logic clients are applying is evident: if AI assists the work, it must cost less. The reality is more complex. Eighty-three percent of search specialists say clients increasingly hire them for advice and expertise rather than pure execution—a finding that contradicts the assumption that AI makes the work simpler or cheaper to deliver.

This tension points to a market education gap. Our recommendation: if you are hiring search freelancers or agencies, audit whether you are pricing based on the tool involved or the outcome achieved. A GEO strategy that lands your brand in AI answers is not cheaper because it uses AI; it is more valuable because it solves a harder problem. Fifty-one percent of search specialists report that businesses underestimate how long search work takes to show results. This belief has not changed, yet pricing pressure has intensified.

## Methodology gaps limit the survey's reliability

[Fiverr's 2026 report omits critical details: total sample size, number of search specialists, survey dates, exact question wording, and whether results were weighted by country](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/fiverr-survey-search-freelancers-geo-demand/590403/). The survey was conducted "ahead of the 2026 holiday season" across eight countries, but no granular timeline is provided. Earlier Fiverr surveys, such as their 2024 holiday index, offered sample sizes and precise collection dates when conducted with research firm Censuswide. This report does not.

We cannot yet verify whether the twenty-nine percent GEO marketplace growth figure was derived from the same search-based methodology Fiverr used for their June Business Trends Index—which compared searches from May to October 2025 against November 2025 to April 2026. The report does not clarify. [Search Engine Journal notes that Fiverr has not responded to requests for this information](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/fiverr-survey-search-freelancers-geo-demand/590403/). When interpreting survey results, methodology transparency matters. Self-reported data from freelancers differs from marketplace-wide trend data, and conflating the two can mask important patterns.

## Tension between execution and advice-seeking work

Eighty-three percent of search specialists report that clients increasingly hire them for strategy and expertise rather than execution alone. This pattern is stronger among search freelancers than among e-commerce (seventy-nine percent) or UGC specialists (seventy-six percent). The pattern suggests that GEO and SEO work are becoming more advisory—clients want to understand the strategy, not just receive deliverables. Our recommendation: if you are outsourcing search work, allocate time for strategy conversations with your freelancer or agency. The work itself may be AI-assisted, but the judgment calls about where to focus effort still require human expertise.

Project costs cluster around fifty-one percent of search specialists reporting fees between one hundred fifty and three hundred dollars per project. Without context on project scope or depth, this figure alone offers limited insight. A hypothesis: this price range may reflect smaller, tactical projects (keyword research, content audit, brief consultation) rather than comprehensive strategy engagements.

## What the survey does not measure

The survey captures client requests, freelancer perceptions, and pricing dynamics. It does not measure whether GEO work actually leads to visibility in AI answers or whether it produces business results. [Compare this to Search Engine Journal's State of Search 2027 report](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/fiverr-survey-search-freelancers-geo-demand/590403/), which surveyed search professionals about their own investment plans and results: forty-three percent plan to invest in or prioritize GEO, and fourteen percent reported that optimization for AI answer engines contributed to their best results. The two surveys ask different questions of different audiences, so their numbers cannot be set directly against each other. Both, however, confirm that GEO is climbing the agenda.

Eighty-nine percent of surveyed search specialists said clients prioritize results over whether AI was involved in the work. This contradicts the assumption that clients are simply chasing a trend; they want outcomes. Yet sixty-one percent report that the mention of AI involvement triggers price-cutting. A hypothesis: clients want results and are willing to pay for them, but they interpret AI involvement as a signal of reduced labor and cost, not increased sophistication.
