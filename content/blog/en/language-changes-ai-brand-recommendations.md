When a customer in Singapore asks an AI chatbot a buying question in Simplified Chinese, and a customer in Kazakhstan asks the same question in Russian, they will not receive the same brand recommendation. [According to reporting in Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/lost-in-translation-how-language-changes-which-brand-ai-recommends/591585/), the language used to query an AI model shapes which brands appear in its response. The finding comes from original research that tested Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini across multiple buying scenarios in five languages. The effect is consistent: asking the same question in different languages leads to different brand recommendations from the same model.

This matters because you are almost certainly tracking your AI visibility only in English. If your customers speak other languages, you are blind to how your brand appears—or fails to appear—in their AI responses.

## Why language changes which brand AI recommends

Language doesn't simply translate recommendations; it changes how AI models reason. [According to the Search Engine Journal investigation](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/lost-in-translation-how-language-changes-which-brand-ai-recommends/591585/), when an AI is addressed in a particular language, it reasons and composes its response in that language, bringing cultural values and reasoning patterns that differ from the English versions. Anthropic published research in July showing that Claude's responses shifted in tone and approach depending on language: leaning toward warmth in Hindi and Arabic, toward rigor in English and Russian. These aren't cosmetic differences. They affect which factors a model weighs when recommending a product or service, and therefore which brands surface in the answer.

The language effect is not incidental. ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are the dominant AI referral sources in markets where English is not primary. In Saudi Arabia, Kazakhstan, and Singapore, these three U.S.-based models accounted for more than 90% of AI referrals. Yet most brand teams have never tested their AI visibility in any language but English.

## How the research tested brand recommendations across languages

The investigator ran 1,200 responses across six shopping and booking scenarios—hotel bookings, flight reservations, e-commerce and productivity tools—using Claude Sonnet 5 in five languages. Each scenario was tested 40 times per language with web search turned off, to isolate the model's training data rather than real-time search results. Additional tests used ChatGPT and Gemini on two scenarios, and re-ran the same scenarios with web search enabled to measure whether live search changed the language effect.

Brand mentions were extracted using text matching. Most brand names rendered in Latin script even in Arabic, Russian, Chinese, and Persian responses. Airlines required special handling: some responses used local-language forms like "英国航空" for British Airways, requiring additional pattern matching. The method resolved cleanly for 99.8% of responses. Our recommendation is to apply the same rigor if you decide to audit your own multilingual visibility: [use a working method for tracking brand mentions across models](/blog/track-brand-mentions-across-llms).

## The multilingual visibility gap your competitors may already know

One detail the Anthropic research did not measure was whether language choice affects whether your brand is mentioned at all. [The Search Engine Journal investigation tested this question directly](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/lost-in-translation-how-language-changes-which-brand-ai-recommends/591585/), and the answer is yes. Different languages produced different winners in the same category.

The implications depend on where your customers live and what languages they speak natively. This is not a problem only for international brands. More than half of residents in Los Angeles County speak another language at home, including around 74,000 Persian speakers. They do not switch to English every time they open ChatGPT. If your brand's AI visibility has never been tested in those languages, you do not know whether you are cited or recommended to them.

Our recommendation is to map your customer base by language and primary market, then test your AI visibility in the languages they actually use. A hypothesis worth testing: brands that show up in English recommendations may rank lower or disappear entirely when the same question is asked in another language.

## Regional AI models complicate the picture further

In China, ChatGPT and Gemini are not accessible at all. Claude is not officially offered in mainland China, Russia, or Iran. Instead, your brand's visibility in those markets depends on regional models—Baidu's ERNIE in China, Naver in South Korea, Mistral in France. [As Search Engine Journal reports](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/lost-in-translation-how-language-changes-which-brand-ai-recommends/591585/), each of these models evolved from a different starting point: a national corpus, a specific cultural identity, and in some cases a government mandate.

This creates a second layer of complexity. Your AI visibility strategy for Arabic speakers may need to account for Western models like Claude and ChatGPT, but also for regional alternatives that may rank your competitors differently. A hypothesis: regional models may weight local brands or content sources differently than Western models, even for the same product category.

The practical consequence: if you sell to customers in multiple countries, your AI visibility audit needs to account for both language and the specific models available in that region.

## What to do about multilingual AI visibility

Start with these steps:

- Identify the countries and languages where your customers conduct purchasing research
- Test your brand's AI visibility in each language using the same query scenarios your customers would use
- Compare the results to your English-language baseline to measure the gap
- Prioritize languages where visibility drops significantly or where competitors gain ground
- Adapt your content strategy to address the [factors that shape AI recommendations](/blog/why-ai-recommends-your-competitors), accounting for cultural values and reasoning patterns in each language
- For major markets, research which AI models dominate (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or regional alternatives) and test visibility in each

A hypothesis: teams that audit multilingual AI visibility before their competitors will uncover citation gaps and brand recommendation shifts that others have not yet measured.

The risk of ignoring this is straightforward. If half your customers ask AI chatbots in a language other than English, and you have only tested visibility in English, you are operating with incomplete data about how many customers see your brand at all.
