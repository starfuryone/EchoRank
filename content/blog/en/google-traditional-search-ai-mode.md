Google is testing whether traditional Search campaigns using exact and phrase match keywords can serve text ads in AI Mode, [according to Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-tests-traditional-search-campaigns-in-ai-mode-487199). This experiment expands which campaign types can appear in Google's AI-powered search experience. Advertisers using exact or phrase match keywords may now reach AI Mode audiences without switching to AI Max or Performance Max—but only when Google's systems detect explicit and direct user intent. The test is small and not yet permanent, but it signals Google is testing how traditional keyword-based advertising fits alongside its more automated campaign products.

## What Google is testing in AI Mode

Google confirmed that exact and phrase match keywords can trigger text ads in AI Mode when a user's query shows explicit and direct intent. [Google Ads Liaison Ginny Marvin confirmed the experiment](https://searchengineland.com/google-tests-traditional-search-campaigns-in-ai-mode-487199), which was first spotted by AdSquire founder Anthony Higman across multiple campaigns. This is not an unrestricted rollout. Eligibility depends entirely on Google's determination that the user's search intent is clear and maps directly to an advertiser's keyword.

The distinction matters. Advertisers are not seeing traditional Search ads appear randomly in AI Mode. Instead, Google's systems are testing whether tightly targeted keywords can serve when a user's intent is unambiguous. A hypothesis is that this approach lets Google balance two competing goals: keeping AI Mode conversational and accessible, while giving advertisers with restrictive match types a path to visibility they would otherwise miss.

## Why this changes the AI Mode calculus for advertisers

Our recommendation is to stop assuming AI Max or Performance Max are the only paths to AI Mode visibility. If you run traditional Search campaigns with exact or phrase match keywords, you may already be getting some AI Mode impressions without knowing it. This matters because it preserves keyword-level control that more automated products strip away.

AI Max and Performance Max are designed for complex, conversational searches and grant access to newer ad formats like Highlighted Answers. Traditional Search campaigns in this test are limited to text ads tied to clearer intent. The tradeoff is less reach against more predictable keyword matching. Our recommendation is to audit your exact and phrase match campaigns for queries that map to clear, direct user needs. If your keywords align with explicit intent, you may already be visible in AI Mode.

A hypothesis is that Google is using this test to measure whether keyword-level precision still matters in AI Mode, or whether it wants to phase keyword control out entirely. We cannot yet verify what volume of queries or impressions this test actually affects.

## What site owners and marketers need to track

Your visibility in AI Mode now potentially includes two channels: campaigns explicitly built for AI (AI Max, Performance Max) and traditional campaigns serving when intent is unmistakable. This complicates measurement. Our recommendation is to:

- Treat AI Mode impressions as a separate reporting category, not as an extension of traditional Search
- Monitor whether traditional Search campaign volume changes in your Google Ads dashboards
- Track which queries and keywords trigger AI Mode appearance, using Google's search query reports
- Audit your content to ensure it can surface in AI-generated answers, not just ads

## The bigger picture: keyword targeting in an AI-first search

Google's search experience is splitting into two parallel systems. One is traditional keyword-based advertising and ranking. The other is AI-powered, conversational, and designed to answer questions directly. This test suggests Google sees value in both, rather than replacing keyword matching entirely.

[Google published a new resource for optimizing content in generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/05/a-new-resource-for-optimizing), reinforcing its position that AI Mode is not a separate Google, but an extension of existing Search. The test of traditional Search campaigns in AI Mode fits that narrative: advertisers and site owners do not need to abandon keyword strategy to be visible in AI.

A hypothesis is that Google is learning how tight the keyword-to-intent mapping must be for traditional ads to work in AI Mode. Is every phrase match eligible, or only those with minimal ambiguity? The test is small and ongoing, so Google may be measuring precision at different thresholds.

## What remains unknown

Google has not disclosed how broadly this test is running, how many advertisers are affected, or what percentage of AI Mode queries it represents. Our recommendation is to treat this as an early signal, not a permanent feature. The company's silence on rollout timelines and eligibility criteria suggests the test may inform a larger decision about whether keyword-based advertising has a future in AI Mode.

It is also unknown whether this test applies only to Google Search or whether it extends to other AI-powered products like Google's generative AI summaries. A hypothesis is that Google is testing keyword-based ads in AI Mode for Search first, then deciding whether the model works elsewhere.

## Key actions for now

- Monitor your traditional Search campaign performance for changes in impression volume or placement
- Use Google's search query reports to identify which queries are triggering AI Mode appearance
- Ensure your site structure and content support both keyword-based Search ranking and AI-generated summaries
- Avoid migrating campaigns to AI Max or Performance Max automatically; test the waters with existing campaigns first

The test is small, but the question it answers will shape how advertisers allocate budgets for the next several years. Our recommendation is to stay alert and measure, rather than overhaul, for now.
